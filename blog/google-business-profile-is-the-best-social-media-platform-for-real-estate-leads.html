Google Business Profile Is the Best Social Media Platform for Real Estate Leads

TRENT STONEHOUSE13 MIN READMARCH 31, 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

Google Business Profile is the highest-converting lead source for local real estate agents — yet most agents ignore it completely. While you chase Instagram followers, buyers and sellers in your market are searching Google right now. A fully optimized GBP with weekly posts, keyword-rich review responses, and a complete services section can put you in front of high-intent leads within 60 seconds of a search.

Every real estate agent has an Instagram strategy. Post Reels, use trending audio, engage in DMs, go live on Thursdays. The whole playbook.

But here is the thing nobody talks about: Instagram followers rarely turn into closings. You are building an audience of other agents, mortgage brokers, and people who liked your kitchen photo but will never buy a house from you.

Meanwhile, Google Business Profile sits there collecting dust. No posts. No updated photos. A handful of reviews from 2022. And you wonder why leads from Google dried up.

Google Business Profile is a social media platform. It has posts. It has photos. It has engagement metrics. It has a feed. And unlike Instagram, the people seeing your content are actively searching for a real estate agent in your city — right now, today.

46% of all Google searches have local intent. That means nearly half of every search on the planet is someone looking for a business or service near them — including real estate agents.

Source: Search Engine Journal, Local SEO Statistics

Why Google Business Profile Is a Social Media Platform

Most agents treat Google Business Profile like a digital business card. You set it up once, maybe add a profile photo, and forget about it. That is a mistake that costs you leads every single day.

GBP has every feature that makes a platform “social”:

  • Posts — You can publish updates, offers, events, and photos directly to your profile, just like any social feed
  • Photos and videos — Visual content appears in your listing and Google Maps results
  • Reviews and responses — Two-way engagement between you and your audience (your clients)
  • Q&A section — People ask questions publicly and you answer them
  • Messaging — Direct messages from potential leads right through the platform
  • Insights and analytics — Track views, clicks, calls, and direction requests

The difference is who is looking. On Instagram, people scroll past your content between memes and vacation photos. On Google, someone typed “real estate agent in [your city]” and your profile appeared. That is the difference between interruption marketing and intent-based marketing.

Key Concept

Intent-based marketing means your content appears when someone is actively searching for what you offer. Google Business Profile captures buyers and sellers at the exact moment they need an agent — no algorithm games, no trending audio required.

When you post on GBP weekly, Google sees your profile as active. Active profiles rank higher in local search results. Higher rankings mean more visibility in the Local 3-Pack — the top three map results that capture the majority of clicks.

The Google Local 3-Pack receives approximately 42% of all clicks on local search results pages. If you are not in those top three spots, you are invisible to nearly half of all local searchers.

Source: Backlinko, Local SEO Statistics

Why Agents Post on Instagram but Ignore GBP

The answer is simple: Instagram feels productive. You get likes. You get comments. You see follower counts go up. It gives you a dopamine hit that makes you think your marketing is working.

But here is what agents miss:

  • Instagram reach is declining. The average organic reach for a business post on Instagram is 9.8% of your followers. If you have 1,000 followers, roughly 98 people see your post.
  • Followers do not equal leads. Most of your Instagram followers are other agents, lenders, title reps, and people outside your market. They will never hire you.
  • The algorithm is unpredictable. One month your Reels get 10,000 views. Next month, 200. You have no control over distribution.
  • There is no search intent. Nobody opens Instagram and searches “real estate agent in Dallas.” They are browsing, not buying.

Compare that to Google Business Profile:

  • 100% intent-based. Every view comes from someone actively searching for what you do
  • No algorithm games. Ranking factors are known and controllable (proximity, relevance, activity)
  • Leads call directly. Your phone number, website, and directions are one tap away
  • Reviews build compound trust. Unlike social proof on Instagram, Google reviews persist forever and influence every future searcher

This is not an argument to delete Instagram. Keep posting there. But stop treating GBP as an afterthought when it is your highest-ROI lead generation platform.

The Full Google Business Profile Optimization Checklist

This is the complete checklist to rank number one in your local market on Google. Every step is actionable. Most agents skip half of these, which is exactly why there is opportunity for you.

Six steps. Follow them in order. Each one builds on the last.

Step 1: Claim and Verify Your Google Business Profile

This sounds obvious, but a surprising number of agents have never actually verified their profile. Without verification, you have zero control over what shows up when someone searches your name or your business.

  1. Go to business.google.com

    Sign in with the Google account you want to manage the profile from. If you already have a listing, claim it. If not, create a new one.

  2. Enter your business information

    Business name, address (use your brokerage address if you work from home), phone number, website, and category. Select “Real estate agent” as your primary category.

  3. Complete verification

    Google typically offers video verification, phone verification, or postcard verification. Video is fastest — record a short clip of your office or brokerage with your business name visible. Most agents get verified within 48 hours.

  4. Fill out every single field

    Hours of operation, service area, business description, attributes (women-led, veteran-owned, etc.), and appointment links. Google rewards completeness. A 100% complete profile outranks an 60% complete profile every time.

Pro Tip

Add secondary categories beyond “Real estate agent.” Include “Real estate consultant,” “Real estate appraiser” (if applicable), “Property management company” (if applicable), and “Real estate agency.” Each category expands the searches you appear in.

Step 2: Optimize Your Business Name

Your Google Business Profile name is one of the strongest ranking signals. Here is the strategy most agents miss.

Google technically says your business name should match your real-world signage. But within those guidelines, there is room to be strategic. Many agents list their name as “John Smith” when they could list it as “John Smith | Dallas Real Estate Agent” — assuming that matches their branding.

What Works

  • Include your city and specialty if it genuinely matches your DBA or branding: “Jane Doe Realty — Austin Luxury Homes”
  • Match your other online listings. Your GBP name should be consistent with your website, social profiles, and brokerage listing. Google cross-references these.
  • Pack in relevant keywords. “John Smith | Dallas Real Estate Agent | Homes for Sale Dallas TX” tells Google exactly what you do and where. The more keyword signals in your name, the stronger your ranking.

What to Avoid

  • Adding phone numbers or URLs to your business name
  • Using ALL CAPS or special characters
  • Including promotional language like “#1 Rated” or “Best Price”
  • Changing your name frequently — Google tracks edits and may suspend profiles that change names often

The business name alone will not get you to number one. But combined with the other five factors, it contributes significantly to your local ranking.

Step 3: Build Out Your Services Section

This is the step that 97% of agents completely skip — and it is one of the easiest wins in local SEO. Your services section is essentially an unlimited keyword field that tells Google exactly what you do and where you do it.

Most agents have zero services listed. Some have two or three generic ones like “Home Buying” and “Home Selling.” That is not enough.

How to Build a Comprehensive Service List

Think about every way a potential client might search for what you do. Each variation becomes a service entry:

  • Buyer services: First-time home buyer agent, buyer consultation, home search assistance, FHA loan specialist, VA loan specialist, relocation services
  • Seller services: Home valuation, listing agent, comparative market analysis, staging consultation, luxury home listing, for-sale-by-owner consultation
  • Specialized services: Investment property analysis, 1031 exchange guidance, estate sales, foreclosure specialist, new construction agent
  • Location variations: “[Neighborhood] real estate agent,” “Homes for sale in [Subdivision],” “[City] luxury homes”

“Near me” searches have grown over 500% in recent years. When someone searches “real estate agent near me,” Google matches them with profiles that have the most relevant, complete information — including the services section.

Source: Think with Google, Near Me Search Trends

Use Google Gemini to Generate Your Services

Here is the shortcut. Open Google Gemini and use this prompt:

Copy This Prompt

“List 50 services a real estate agent in [your city] would offer. Include buyer services, seller services, specialized services, property types, and location-specific variations. Format each as a short service name suitable for a Google Business Profile listing.”

Gemini understands Google’s search patterns because it is trained on Google Search data. The services it suggests align with how real people actually search — not how agents think they search. Run the prompt, review the output, remove anything that does not apply, and add your specific neighborhoods.

Step 4: Build a Reviews Strategy That Compounds

Reviews are the single most visible trust signal on your Google profile. A profile with 5 reviews and a profile with 50 reviews are not even in the same conversation — both in terms of ranking and in terms of consumer trust.

The 25-Review Baseline

Your first target is 25 reviews. That is the threshold where your profile starts to feel credible. Below 25, you look new or inactive. Above 25, you look established and trusted. Above 50, you dominate.

How to Ask for Reviews

  • Text, do not email. Text messages have a 98% open rate. Emails sit in spam folders. Send a direct link to your Google review page via text within 24 hours of a positive interaction.
  • Make it specific. Instead of “Can you leave me a review?” say “Would you mind sharing your experience working with me on the [Street Name] sale? Here is the link.”
  • Ask at the right moment. The best time to ask is right after a successful closing, after you solve a problem, or after a client compliments your work. Emotion drives action.
  • Follow up once. If they do not leave a review within three days, send one polite follow-up. Do not nag.

For New Agents Without Clients

If you are new and have zero closings, you can still build reviews. Ask people you have helped in any capacity — friends you gave market advice to, neighbors you helped with home value questions, anyone who has experienced your knowledge firsthand. Character reviews are legitimate and get you to that initial threshold while you build your client base.

88% of consumers trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations. Your Google reviews are not just a vanity metric — they are the first thing potential clients evaluate before deciding to contact you.

Source: BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey

Step 5: Write Keyword-Rich Review Responses

This is the step that separates agents who rank from agents who do not. Every single review response is an SEO opportunity. Google indexes your responses, which means the keywords you use in them influence your ranking.

The Holy Trinity of Keywords

Every review response should naturally include three elements:

  1. Your name — reinforces your personal brand in search results
  2. Your city or neighborhood — tells Google where you operate
  3. Your service — tells Google what you do

Example Responses

Good Response Example

“Thank you so much, Sarah! Helping you find your first home in Frisco was one of my favorite closings this year. As a Frisco real estate agent, I love working with first-time buyers who are ready to invest in this community. If anyone in Frisco needs a buyer’s agent, I’d love to help. Congratulations again!”

That single response naturally includes your name (in your profile), “Frisco” (three times), “real estate agent,” “first-time buyers,” and “buyer’s agent.” Google reads every word.

Go Back and Edit Old Responses

Here is a move most agents do not realize: you can edit your old review responses. Go back through every review you have ever responded to and rewrite those responses with keyword-rich language. This retroactively improves your profile’s SEO without waiting for new reviews.

If your old responses say “Thanks for the review!” — rewrite them. That generic response is doing nothing for your ranking. Every response should work for you 24 hours a day.

“Your review responses are free real estate on Google. Every one of them is an SEO opportunity that most agents waste with two-word replies.”
Shawn Kaplan

Step 6: Post on Google Business Profile Every Week

This is the “social media” part of GBP that almost nobody uses. Google Business Profile has a posting feature. You can publish updates directly to your profile — and they appear in search results when someone looks up your business.

Google tracks how recently you posted. Profiles that post weekly are flagged as active businesses. Active businesses rank higher. It is that straightforward.

What to Post on GBP

  • Market updates — “Home prices in [City] rose 3% this quarter. Here is what that means for buyers.”
  • Just listed / just sold — Showcase your active transactions with photos
  • Local events — Farmers markets, festivals, school events, community news. This signals local relevance to Google.
  • Tips and advice — “3 things first-time buyers in [City] should know before making an offer”
  • Client wins — Celebrate closings (with permission) to show activity and social proof
  • Open houses — GBP has an “Event” post type perfect for open house announcements

GBP Post Best Practices

  • Include a photo with every post. Posts with images get significantly more engagement than text-only posts.
  • Add a call-to-action button. GBP lets you add buttons like “Call now,” “Learn more,” or “Book.” Use them.
  • Keep it short. GBP posts truncate after about 100 words. Lead with the most important information.
  • Use local keywords naturally. Mention your city, neighborhood, or community in each post.
  • Post at least once per week. Consistency matters more than volume. One post per week signals activity. Zero posts for months signals abandonment.

Google Business Profiles that post weekly see 2x more website visits compared to profiles that post monthly or not at all. Weekly activity is the minimum threshold that tells Google your business is active and relevant.

Source: Sterling Sky, GBP Post Frequency Study

Here is the bottom line: you are already creating social media content for Instagram and Facebook. Take that same content — the market update, the just-sold post, the tip carousel — and repurpose it as a GBP post. It takes two minutes and the lead quality is exponentially higher.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google Business Profile really free?Yes, completely free. Claiming, verifying, and managing your Google Business Profile costs nothing. You can add photos, post updates, respond to reviews, and manage your listing without spending a dollar. Google makes money from ads — the organic profile is a free tool for businesses.
How long does it take to rank in the Local 3-Pack?It depends on your market competition. In smaller cities, you can see results within 2-4 weeks of fully optimizing your profile. In competitive metros like Dallas, LA, or Miami, it can take 2-3 months of consistent effort — weekly posts, new reviews, keyword-rich responses, and a complete services section. Proximity to the searcher is also a major factor you cannot control.
Can I use the same content on GBP and Instagram?Absolutely, and you should. A market update you post on Instagram works just as well as a GBP post. The key difference is adding local keywords and a call-to-action button when you post on GBP.
Should I respond to negative reviews on Google?Always. Respond professionally, acknowledge the concern, and offer to resolve it offline. Never argue or get defensive in a public response. Potential clients reading your reviews will judge you more on how you handle criticism than on the negative review itself. And yes — use keywords naturally in your response to negative reviews too.
Do GBP posts expire or disappear?Google used to archive posts after 7 days, but that policy has changed. Posts now remain visible on your profile indefinitely, though the most recent posts get the most visibility. This is another reason to post weekly — fresh content stays at the top of your profile and signals to Google that your business is actively operating.
