The Google Business Profile Section 97% of Agents Ignore

It’s called the Services section. And it’s hiding in plain sight.

When you log into your Google Business Profile and click “Edit profile,” you’ll see tabs for basic info, hours, photos, and more. Most agents fill out the basics and stop. But there’s a dedicated Services tab that lets you list every single service you offer — and Google indexes all of them.

Here’s why this matters: Google uses your Services section to understand what you do. When someone searches “first-time home buyer agent in Phoenix” or “luxury listing specialist near me,” Google checks your profile to see if those terms match your listed services. If your Services section is blank, you’re invisible for those searches.

What Is the GBP Services Section?

The Services section in Google Business Profile lets you list individual services your business provides. Each service includes a name and an optional description (up to 300 characters). Google uses these entries as relevance signals when matching your profile to local searches. Unlike your business description (which has a 750-character limit), the Services section has no limit on the number of entries.

Think of every service you add as a free keyword you’re telling Google about. Most agents have zero services listed. The agents dominating local search have 50, 80, even 100+.

Businesses with complete Google Business Profiles are 70% more likely to attract location visits and 50% more likely to lead to a purchase. The Services section is one of the most impactful — and most neglected — completion fields.

Source: Google Business Profile Help Documentation

How the Services Section Works (And Why It’s Free SEO)

The Services section is structured SEO that Google reads directly. Unlike your business description — which is limited, generic, and often ignored by the algorithm — Services entries are treated as individual relevance signals.

Here’s how it works technically:

  • Each service entry has a name field — this is the keyword. “First-Time Home Buyer Representation” tells Google you serve first-time buyers.
  • Each entry has an optional description — up to 300 characters. This is where you add location-specific keywords and context.
  • Services are grouped by category — Google lets you create custom categories to organize your services logically.
  • There is no cap on entries — you can add 10 or 200. More relevant services = more keyword coverage.

When someone searches “relocation agent in Austin TX,” Google scans your profile. If “Relocation Services — Austin TX” is listed as a service, you just became more relevant for that search. It’s that direct.

How to Turn Services Into Unlimited Keywords

This is where the strategy gets powerful. Instead of thinking “What services do I offer?” think “What would someone type into Google when they need me?”

Every service entry should be written as a search query, not a job title. Here’s the difference:

Weak vs. Strong Service Entries

Weak: “Home Sales” — vague, no location, no specificity.

Strong: “Luxury Home Sales in Scottsdale AZ” — specific service + location + search intent. This matches how real people actually search Google.

Keyword Categories to Cover

Build your service list across these categories to maximize coverage:

  • Buyer services: First-time home buyer representation, VA loan home buying assistance, investment property acquisition, buyer consultation, relocation buyer services
  • Seller services: Home listing and marketing, pre-sale home staging consultation, comparative market analysis, luxury listing marketing, FSBO conversion assistance
  • Property types: Single-family home sales, condo and townhouse sales, new construction representation, luxury estate sales, multi-family investment properties
  • Specialized services: Divorce real estate specialist, estate and probate sales, 1031 exchange guidance, short sale negotiation, foreclosure assistance
  • Location-specific: Add your city, county, and neighborhood names to each service. “Home Buyer Agent in [City]” — repeat for every area you serve.

“Near me” searches have grown 500% in the last two years. When someone searches “real estate agent near me,” Google matches them with profiles that have the most relevant, complete information — including services.

Source: Think with Google, Near Me Search Trends

The goal is to cover every possible way someone might search for what you do. A buyer might search “first-time buyer agent” or “help buying my first home” or “FHA loan realtor near me.” Each variation should be a service entry.

Use Google Gemini to Generate Your Service List

Here’s the hack that makes this take 5 minutes instead of an hour. Google’s own AI — Gemini — can generate a comprehensive list of real estate services tailored to your market. And because it’s Google’s AI, it already understands what Google indexes and values.

The Exact Prompt to Use

Copy This Gemini Prompt

“List 50 services a real estate agent in [your city] would offer. Include buyer services, seller services, specialized services, property types, and location-specific variations. Format each as a short service name suitable for a Google Business Profile listing.”

Replace “[your city]” with your actual market. Gemini will return a detailed list of 50+ services, already formatted for your profile. You can run the prompt multiple times with slight variations to get even more:

  • “List 30 niche real estate services for [city]” — gets specialized services like estate sales, 1031 exchanges, relocation
  • “List 20 property-type-specific services for a realtor in [city]” — gets condos, townhomes, luxury, land, commercial
  • “List neighborhood-specific real estate services for [city]” — gets hyper-local entries like “Home buyer agent in [Neighborhood Name]”

Using Google’s own AI to optimize your Google profile is a strategic advantage. Gemini understands Google’s search intent framework, so the services it suggests align with how people actually search — not how agents think they search.

Why Gemini Over ChatGPT for This Task

You can use any AI tool, but Gemini has a specific advantage here: it’s trained on Google Search data. It knows which terms people actually search for on Google. When it suggests “Luxury Home Staging Consultation in [City]” as a service, it’s pulling from real search patterns — not guessing.

That said, the real power is in running the prompt, reviewing the output, and customizing it for your market. Don’t just copy and paste blindly. Remove anything that doesn’t apply to your business, add your specific neighborhoods, and make sure every entry reflects a service you actually provide or can credibly offer.

Step-by-Step: Adding Services to Your Profile

This takes about 15–30 minutes and is one of the highest-ROI activities you can do for your local SEO. Here’s the exact process.

  1. Open Google Business Profile Manager

    Go to business.google.com or search your business name on Google and click “Edit profile” from the Knowledge Panel. Make sure you’re logged into the Google account that owns the profile.

  2. Navigate to the Services Tab

    Inside the profile editor, look for the “Services” tab. On desktop, it’s in the left sidebar. On mobile, scroll through the tabs at the top. If you don’t see it, make sure your primary category is set to “Real estate agent” or a related category.

  3. Create Service Categories

    Click “Add a section” or “Add custom service.” Create logical groupings: Buyer Services, Seller Services, Specialized Services, Property Types, Neighborhood Services. This keeps your list organized and easy for Google to parse.

  4. Add Individual Services

    For each category, start adding services. Enter the service name (your keyword) and an optional description (up to 300 characters). Use the description to include your city, county, or neighborhood name for extra local relevance.

  5. Paste Your Gemini-Generated List

    Open Gemini, run the prompt from the previous section, and start adding each service. You’ll need to enter them one at a time, but it goes fast. Aim for 50+ services on your first pass. You can always add more later.

  6. Save and Verify

    Click “Save” after each section. Google may take 24–72 hours to index your new services. Check back in a week and search for some of the services you added to see if your profile appears in results.

Why Your Category Selection Matters

Before you touch the Services section, make sure your profile categories are set correctly. Your primary and secondary categories determine which Services options Google shows you — and which searches you’re eligible to appear in.

Primary Category

Your primary category should be “Real estate agent” in most cases. This is the single most important ranking signal for your profile. Google weighs the primary category more heavily than anything else when deciding which profiles to show in the Local 3-Pack.

Secondary Categories

Add every relevant secondary category. Google allows up to 9 additional categories. Here are the ones that matter for real estate:

  • Real estate agency — if you have a team or brokerage
  • Real estate consultant — for advisory and consulting services
  • Property management company — if you manage rentals
  • Real estate appraiser — if you offer CMA or appraisal support
  • Apartment building or Condominium complex — if you specialize in these property types

Your primary category is the #1 ranking factor in the Local Pack, according to Moz’s annual Local Search Ranking Factors study. Agents who add relevant secondary categories see an average 15–20% increase in search visibility for related queries.

Source: Moz, Local Search Ranking Factors, 2025

The combination of the right categories plus a filled-out Services section is what tells Google: “This agent does [X] in [Y location].” Without both, you’re leaving ranking signals on the table.

How to Check Your Current Categories

  1. Search your business name on Google

    Your Knowledge Panel will show your primary category under your business name. If it says anything other than “Real estate agent” (like “Real estate agency”), consider changing it.

  2. Go to Edit Profile → Business Category

    Here you can see all your categories. Add any relevant ones you’re missing. Each additional category opens up new keyword opportunities in the Services section.

What Happens After You Add Services

Here’s what you can expect after filling out your Services section properly.

Week 1–2: Google Indexes Your Services

Google typically processes profile updates within 24–72 hours, but the full indexing and ranking impact takes 1–2 weeks. During this time, your profile is being re-evaluated for a broader range of search queries.

Week 2–4: Impressions Start Climbing

Check your Google Business Profile Insights. You should see an increase in “Search queries” — the terms people used to find your profile. If you added 50+ services, you’ll start appearing for search terms you never showed up for before.

Month 2–3: Calls and Clicks Increase

As your profile appears in more searches, the downstream metrics follow: more website clicks, more direction requests, more phone calls. The compounding effect is real. Each service entry is a door that a potential client can walk through to find you.

Businesses that fully optimize their Google Business Profile receive 7x more clicks than those with incomplete profiles. Adding services is one of the fastest ways to move from “incomplete” to “fully optimized” in Google’s eyes.

Source: Google Business Profile Help

The Compound Effect

Here’s what most agents miss about local SEO: every optimization you make compounds over time. Adding services today makes your profile more relevant. More relevance means more impressions. More impressions mean more clicks and calls. More calls mean more reviews. More reviews mean higher rankings. And the cycle continues.

The agents who own local search didn’t do one big thing. They did 20 small things — services, categories, photos, reviews, posts — and let the compounding effect do the work.
