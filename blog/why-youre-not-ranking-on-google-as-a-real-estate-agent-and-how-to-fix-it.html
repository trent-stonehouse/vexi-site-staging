Why You’re Not Ranking on Google as a Real Estate Agent (And How to Fix It)

TRENT STONEHOUSE6 MIN READMARCH 30, 2026

Google ranks real estate agents based on three factors: proximity, relevance, and activity. Most agents fail on at least two of them — and they don’t even know it. Here’s exactly what Google looks at, why you’re probably invisible right now, and how to fix it.

Why Google Decides Who Shows Up First for Real Estate Searches

Every time someone searches “realtor near me” or “real estate agent in Nashville,” Google runs a split-second decision based on three signals. Your ranking — whether you’re in the top 3 results or buried on page 4 — comes down entirely to how well your profile matches these factors.

According to Google, 46% of all searches have local intent. And 8.5 billion searches happen on Google every day. That means your local market is actively searching — the question is whether they find you or your competitor.

Factor #1: Proximity — Why Your Address Is Everything

Google’s first ranking factor is proximity: how close your business address is to the person searching.

This is why your Google Business Profile address matters more than almost anything else. If your address is in Franklin, TN, you will dominate Franklin searches. You will not dominate Nashville searches — no matter how good your profile is.

What this means for you:

  • Focus all your SEO energy on your primary city — the city where your address is located
  • You can still mention nearby cities in your Services section to pick up some visibility there
  • If you want to dominate multiple cities, you need a verified address in each one
  • Trying to rank in a big city 45 minutes away? Stop. You’re wasting your time.

The agents who try to rank everywhere end up ranking nowhere. Pick your city. Own it.

Pro Tip: If you work from home, consider a co-working space or virtual office in your target area. Google accepts virtual office addresses for service-area businesses, and the proximity boost can be the difference between page 1 and page 4.

Factor #2: Relevance — How Well Your Profile Matches What People Search

Google reads every word on your Google Business Profile and matches it against what people type. If someone searches “realtor in Nashville” and your profile says “realtor in Nashville” 47 times in the right places, Google has no choice but to recommend you.

This is called keyword optimization — and most real estate agents completely ignore it.

Where keywords matter most on your Google Business Profile:

  • Business name: Instead of “Mike Johnson,” try “Mike Johnson — Realtor | Real Estate Agent | Nashville, TN”
  • Services section: Add every keyword phrase a potential client might search — “realtor near me,” “find a realtor,” “best real estate agent in [city].” Use AI to generate 100+ keyword-loaded service phrases.
  • Google Reviews: Ask clients to include keywords in their reviews — “I worked with [name], a realtor in Nashville, to find our dream home.” Reviews with location + role keywords boost your relevance score.
  • Review responses: Respond to every review with keywords injected naturally. “Thank you for trusting me as your Nashville relocation specialist…” You can even edit old responses from years ago.

Factor #3: Activity — Dead Profiles Don’t Rank

Google’s third ranking signal is activity: how recently and consistently your business has been updated.

A Google Business Profile from 2020 with no recent posts? Google assumes you’re no longer in business. A profile updated weekly with posts, new reviews, and fresh photos? Google sees an active, trustworthy business.

What “activity” looks like to Google:

  • Weekly posts on your Google Business Profile (treat it like a social media platform)
  • New reviews coming in consistently — aim for at least 2 per week
  • Updated photos and service listings
  • Responses to every review within 48 hours

The agents ranking in the Google 3-Pack aren’t necessarily the best agents. They’re the most active ones with the most optimized profiles.

Pro Tip: Set a weekly 15-minute calendar reminder to post a Google Business update. Even a short post about a new listing, market stat, or neighborhood highlight tells Google your profile is active — and active profiles consistently outrank dormant ones.

The Google 3-Pack: Why Those Top 3 Spots Win Everything

When someone searches “realtor near me,” the first thing they see is a map with three business listings — called the Google 3-Pack. Those top 3 positions capture 79% of all clicks on the page.

If you’re not in the 3-Pack, you’re essentially invisible. Most people don’t scroll past those three results. They pick one, they call, they hire.

Getting into the 3-Pack requires all three factors working together: your address is in the right city, your profile is loaded with the right keywords, and you’re posting and collecting reviews consistently.

The One Mistake That Kills Most Real Estate Google Rankings

The single most common mistake: agents try to rank in cities where they don’t have an address.

Google’s proximity algorithm is non-negotiable. You can have a perfect profile, hundreds of reviews, and daily posts — but if your address says “Franklin” and someone in Nashville is searching, Nashville agents will outrank you every time.

The fix: focus entirely on dominating the city where your address is. Build an unbeatable profile there. Then expand from a position of strength.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my Google Business Profile not showing up in search results?

Your profile is likely missing in the Google 3-Pack for one of three reasons: your business address is outside the city someone is searching in (proximity), your profile doesn’t contain the keywords people are searching (relevance), or your profile is inactive with no recent posts or reviews (activity). Fix all three to start ranking.

How long does it take to rank on Google as a real estate agent?

Most agents who apply the full keyword system — optimized business name, keyword-loaded services, consistent review collection, and weekly posts — start seeing movement in the Google 3-Pack within 30 to 90 days. Competitive markets take longer; smaller cities move faster.

Do Google reviews really help real estate agents rank higher?

Yes — but only if the reviews contain keywords. “Great experience!” does nothing for your ranking. Reviews that mention your city, your role, and what you helped with (“helped me sell my home in Nashville as a first-time seller”) signal relevance to Google and improve your local search ranking.

Can a real estate agent rank in multiple cities on Google?

Google’s proximity algorithm prioritizes your registered business address. To truly dominate multiple cities, you need a verified address in each one. You can mention nearby cities in your Services section for some supplemental visibility, but you’ll only rank at the top in your primary address city.

What should I post on Google Business Profile as a realtor?

Post consistently — at least once per week. Good content includes recent listings, closed deals with client testimonials, local market updates, neighborhood guides, and tips for buyers and sellers. Treat your Google Business Profile like a social media platform. Keyword-rich posts in the description help boost relevance.

What is the Google 3-Pack and why does it matter for realtors?

The Google 3-Pack is the map section that appears at the top of local search results showing three businesses with their ratings, address, and phone number. Studies show these three positions receive 79% of all clicks on the page. For real estate agents, being in the 3-Pack means your phone rings — being outside it means most searchers never find you.
