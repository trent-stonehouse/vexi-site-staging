The Google Review Template That Boosts Your Local SEO

TRENT STONEHOUSE6 MIN READAPRIL 1, 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

The best google review template for real estate includes the Holy Trinity keywords: Realtor, Real Estate Agent, and your city name. Write 20 pre-written review templates with different keyword variations, send them to clients as examples they can customize, and ask directly for location-specific keywords. Your goal is 25 keyword-loaded reviews. Reviews that say “great experience” are worthless for SEO — reviews that mention your role and city tell Google exactly who you are and where you work.

Your Google reviews are worthless if they don’t have keywords. Most realtors are sitting on dozens of reviews that say “great experience!” and wondering why they’re not ranking. Google doesn’t care about generic praise. Google cares about keywords.

The difference between a review that helps your ranking and one that doesn’t comes down to three words: Realtor, Real Estate Agent, and your city name. That’s the Holy Trinity. Every google review template for real estate should be built around these keywords.

Businesses with keyword-rich reviews rank 25% higher in local search results than those with generic reviews. Google uses review content as a relevance signal for local SEO ranking.

Source: Whitespark, Local Search Ranking Factors, 2025

Why Keywords in Your Google Reviews Matter More Than Star Ratings

A 5-star review that says “great service” does almost nothing for your Google ranking. But a 5-star review that says “John was an amazing realtor who helped us find our dream home in Nashville” tells Google exactly what you do, what you are, and where you do it.

Google’s local search algorithm uses review content as a relevance signal. When someone searches “realtor in Nashville,” Google scans your reviews for those exact keywords. If your reviews mention “realtor” and “Nashville” 25 times, Google has no choice but to consider you relevant for that search.

This is why the google review template for real estate matters so much. You need to guide your clients toward including specific keywords — not because you’re gaming the system, but because clients genuinely want to help. They just don’t know what to write.

The Holy Trinity Keywords

The three most powerful keywords for any real estate agent’s Google reviews are: Realtor, Real Estate Agent, and your city name. These are the exact terms people search when looking for an agent. Every review should contain at least one of these three.

Strategy #1: Send Pre-Written Google Review Templates

Write 20 different review templates with different keywords and send them to your clients. Say: “Here are some examples — feel free to customize.” This makes it easy for them and gets you the keywords you need.

Most clients want to leave you a great review. They just don’t know what to say. When you hand them a google review template for real estate with the keywords already built in, they’ll either use it directly or customize it with their own words — either way, the keywords make it in.

Here are example templates with keyword variations:

  1. First-Time Buyer Template

    “[Name] was the best realtor we could have asked for as first-time home buyers in [City]. They walked us through every step and made the process stress-free. Highly recommend this real estate agent to anyone looking to buy in [City].”

  2. Seller Template

    “We hired [Name] as our real estate agent to sell our home in [City] and couldn’t be happier. They got us above asking price in under 2 weeks. Best realtor in [City] hands down.”

  3. Relocation Template

    “[Name] made our relocation to [City] seamless. As a real estate agent, they knew the neighborhoods, schools, and market inside and out. If you need a realtor in [City], this is who you call.”

Create 20 variations covering different scenarios: first-time buyers, sellers, investors, relocations, luxury homes, downsizers. Each template should naturally include “realtor,” “real estate agent,” and your city name.

Strategy #2: Use a Charity Incentive for Keyword-Rich Reviews

For every keyword your client includes in their review, donate $5 to a local charity. They feel good, you get keywords. It’s a win-win that makes the ask feel generous instead of self-serving.

Here’s how it works in practice:

  • After closing, tell your client: “I’d love a Google review. For every keyword you include — like realtor, real estate agent, or [City] — I’ll donate $5 to [local charity].”
  • Most clients will include 3–5 keywords, which means $15–$25 per review to charity
  • You get keyword-loaded reviews that boost your ranking, and the client feels great about contributing to a cause

This strategy works because it reframes the ask. You’re not asking for a favor — you’re creating a reason for them to be detailed and specific. The more keywords they include, the more impact they have. Everyone wins.

Strategy #3: Just Ask Directly for Keywords

Sometimes the simplest approach works best: just be direct. Say: “Can you mention that I’m a realtor in [City]?” Most people are happy to help — they just don’t know what to write without guidance.

The direct ask works because clients genuinely want to support you. They sat through dozens of showings, weeks of negotiations, and mountains of paperwork with you. Writing a review is the easy part — they just need direction.

At minimum, get one Holy Trinity keyword in every review. Here’s the script:

  • At closing: “I’d really appreciate a Google review. If you could mention that I’m a realtor in [City], that helps me get found by other families like yours.”
  • In your follow-up email: Include the direct link to your Google review page with a note: “Mentioning keywords like realtor, real estate agent, and [City] helps other buyers find me.”
  • With the template: Send 2–3 examples alongside the direct ask so they have both the request and the roadmap

Agents who ask directly for keyword-specific reviews get them 73% of the time. Most clients are willing to include specific terms — they just need to be asked. The barrier isn’t willingness, it’s awareness.

Source: BrightLocal, Consumer Review Survey, 2025

Bonus Keywords to Include Beyond the Holy Trinity

The Holy Trinity — Realtor, Real Estate Agent, your city — covers your base. But if you want to dominate niche searches, ask clients to include bonus keywords that match specific services you offer.

Here are the bonus keywords to weave into your google review template for real estate:

  • First-time home buyer — Huge search volume from new buyers looking for patient, knowledgeable agents
  • Relocation specialist — Captures people moving to your area from out of state
  • Luxury homes — If you work the luxury market, this keyword in reviews signals expertise
  • Downsizing — Growing search term as boomers look to sell and move to smaller homes
  • Specific neighborhoods — Mentioning neighborhoods, school districts, or subdivisions captures hyper-local searches

Your goal is 25 keyword-loaded reviews. Not 25 reviews total — 25 reviews that contain meaningful keywords. If you’re starting from zero, even getting family members to leave character reviews with keywords can kickstart your ranking. Start campaigning for keyword-rich reviews today and watch your local search ranking climb.
