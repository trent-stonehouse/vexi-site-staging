Why You’re Not Ranking on Google as a Real Estate Agent

TRENT STONEHOUSE13 MIN READMARCH 31, 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

Google ranks local businesses using three factors: proximity (how close you are to the searcher), relevance (how well your profile matches the search), and activity (how engaged and active your profile is). Most real estate agents fail at all three because they target too broad an area, leave their profile incomplete, and never post or respond to reviews.

Why You’re Invisible on Google

You set up your Google Business Profile. You added your photo. Maybe you even asked a few clients for reviews. And yet when someone searches “real estate agent near me” in your city, you’re nowhere to be found.

You’re not broken. You’re not unlucky. You’re just missing the three things Google actually uses to decide who shows up and who doesn’t.

Most agents treat Google like a set-it-and-forget-it yellow pages listing. They fill out their profile once, never touch it again, and wonder why they get zero leads from search. The agents who dominate local search treat their Google Business Profile like a living, breathing marketing channel — because that’s exactly what it is.

46% of all Google searches have local intent. That means nearly half the people searching on Google right now are looking for something nearby — including a real estate agent.

Source: Search Engine Journal

If you’re not showing up in those results, you’re handing leads to whoever is. And the gap between ranking and not ranking comes down to three specific factors that Google has told us about directly.

The 3 Google Ranking Factors That Control Your Visibility

Google uses a simple framework to decide which businesses appear in local search results. Every local ranking decision comes down to three signals:

The Google Local Ranking Formula

Proximity (how close you are to the searcher) + Relevance (how well your profile matches the query) + Activity (how engaged and current your profile is) = Your local search ranking.

These three factors work together. You can’t just nail one and ignore the others. But here’s the truth most agents miss: proximity is the dominant factor, and it’s the one you have the least control over. That means relevance and activity become your leverage points.

Let’s break down each factor, what it means for your business, and exactly what to do about it.

Factor 1: Proximity — The Factor You Can’t Change

Proximity is everything. It’s the single biggest factor in whether you show up in local search results, and it’s the one factor you have almost zero control over.

What Proximity Means

Proximity is the physical distance between the searcher’s location and your business address. Google prioritizes businesses that are geographically close to the person searching. If someone searches “realtor near me” from downtown Denver, Google will favor agents with addresses near downtown Denver.

This is why an agent in Scottsdale won’t show up when someone searches from Phoenix, even if their profile is perfect. Google doesn’t care how good your reviews are if you’re 20 miles away from the searcher. Distance trumps quality in local search.

Why Agents Get This Wrong

Most agents list a single office address and then try to target an entire metro area. They want to be the “Denver real estate agent” or the “Dallas Realtor.” But Google doesn’t work that way.

Google serves hyper-local results. When someone in Plano, TX searches for a real estate agent, Google shows agents in Plano — not agents 30 miles away in downtown Dallas who claim to serve the whole DFW metroplex.

The average Google local search result shows businesses within a 5-mile radius of the searcher. For competitive categories like real estate, that radius can shrink to 2-3 miles in dense urban areas.

Source: BrightLocal Local Search Research

How to Work Around Proximity

You can’t change where your office is. But you can make strategic choices about how you position yourself geographically:

  1. Pick your city and own it

    Stop trying to rank for an entire metro. Choose the specific city or neighborhood where your office is located and make every piece of your online presence scream that location. Your GBP, website, content, and reviews should all reinforce one geography.

  2. Use a real street address

    PO boxes and virtual offices do not work for Google Business Profile. You need a verified physical address in the city you want to rank in. If you work from home, that address is eligible as your business location.

  3. Create location-specific landing pages

    Build pages on your website for each neighborhood and suburb you serve. “Homes for sale in [Neighborhood]” pages with unique, useful content help Google associate you with those specific areas even if your office isn’t there.

  4. Get reviews that mention your city

    When clients leave reviews, encourage them to mention the city or neighborhood naturally. “Sarah helped us find our dream home in Chandler” is exponentially more valuable for local SEO than “Great agent, highly recommend.”

Proximity is the biggest factor, but it’s not the only one. The agents who dominate local search accept the proximity limitation and then crush the other two factors to compensate.

Factor 2: Relevance — Tell Google Exactly What You Do

Relevance is how well your Google Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. If your profile is vague, incomplete, or generic, Google has no reason to show you for specific searches.

This is the factor most agents completely botch — and it’s the easiest one to fix.

What Relevance Means

Google compares the searcher’s query against your profile information: business name, categories, services, description, posts, reviews, and website content. The more signals that match the search intent, the higher your relevance score.

Where Agents Lose Relevance Points

Most agents set up their Google Business Profile with the bare minimum and call it done. Here’s what a low-relevance profile looks like:

  • One category selected (“Real estate agent” — and nothing else)
  • No services listed — Google literally gives you a section to list every service you offer, and it’s blank
  • Generic description — “I help buyers and sellers in the greater metro area”
  • No posts — zero content on the profile to signal expertise
  • Reviews with no keywords — “Great agent!” tells Google nothing

Compare that to an agent who has filled out every available field, listed 15+ services, chosen multiple relevant categories, writes keyword-rich posts weekly, and has reviews that mention specific services and locations.

Google rewards completeness. An incomplete profile is an irrelevant profile.

How to Maximize Your Relevance Score

  1. Add every relevant category

    Your primary category should be “Real estate agent.” Then add secondary categories: Real estate agency, Property management company, Real estate consultant, Real estate appraiser (if applicable). Each category expands the searches you can appear in.

  2. List every service you offer

    Go to your GBP dashboard and add services: buyer representation, seller representation, listing agent, first-time homebuyer consultation, relocation services, investment property analysis, market analysis, home valuation, open house coordination. Be thorough.

  3. Write a keyword-rich description

    Your GBP description should include your name, city, services, and specialties. Not keyword-stuffed garbage — natural language that mentions what you do and where you do it. “Sarah Johnson is a residential real estate agent in Chandler, Arizona, specializing in first-time homebuyers and luxury properties.”

  4. Use the Q&A section

    Google lets you pre-populate questions and answers on your profile. Add 10-15 common questions about your services, areas served, and process. Each Q&A is indexed content that boosts relevance for those specific topics.

  5. Match your website to your GBP

    Your website should reinforce every keyword and service listed on your GBP. If your profile says “luxury homes in Scottsdale” but your website never mentions Scottsdale or luxury, Google sees a mismatch and lowers your relevance.

Businesses with complete Google Business Profiles are 70% more likely to attract location visits and 50% more likely to lead to a purchase.

Source: Google Business Profile Help

Factor 3: Activity — Prove You’re Still in Business

Activity is the factor that separates the agents who show up from the agents who used to show up. Google wants to recommend active, engaged businesses. A stale profile signals to Google that you might not even be operating anymore.

What Activity Means

Activity measures how frequently you interact with your Google Business Profile: posting updates, responding to reviews, answering questions, adding photos, updating business information. Google tracks all of it, and profiles with consistent activity get priority in local results.

Think of it this way: if Google has to choose between showing an agent who posted last week and responds to every review within 24 hours, versus an agent who hasn’t touched their profile in 6 months — who do you think they’ll recommend?

What Counts as Activity

Activity Type Impact Recommended Frequency
Google Posts High — fresh content with keywords 2-3 per week minimum
Review responses High — engagement + keyword opportunity Within 24 hours of every review
Photo uploads Medium — signals active business 5-10 new photos per month
Q&A responses Medium — indexed content Answer within 24 hours
Profile updates Low-medium — freshness signal Monthly review and update
New reviews received Very high — social proof + keywords 2-4 new reviews per month

How to Build a Consistent Activity System

  1. Post to Google 2-3 times per week

    Google Posts are free content that appear directly on your profile and in search results. Share market updates, new listings, homebuyer tips, community events, and client success stories. Every post should include your city name and a relevant service keyword.

  2. Respond to every single review

    Not just a “thanks.” Every review response is an SEO opportunity. Include your name, city, and service in each response naturally. This is one of the most overlooked ranking tactics in local SEO.

  3. Upload fresh photos regularly

    Google rewards profiles with recent photo uploads. Add photos of listings, your office, local community events, behind-the-scenes of your business. Geo-tag them when possible for an extra location signal.

  4. Build a review generation system

    Don’t rely on clients randomly leaving reviews. Create a process: send a review request link via text within 24 hours of closing. Make it easy. The agents who dominate local search have a consistent flow of new reviews every month.

The agents who rank on Google aren’t smarter. They’re just more consistent. They post every week, respond to every review, and treat their Google profile like a marketing channel — not a business card.
Trent Stonehouse

The #1 Mistake: Targeting Too Broad an Area

This is the mistake that kills more agents’ Google rankings than anything else. They try to be the agent for an entire metropolitan area instead of owning one specific city.

Here’s the reality: if you’re targeting “Phoenix real estate agent,” you’re competing against thousands of agents. But if you’re targeting “Chandler AZ real estate agent,” the competition drops dramatically — and the leads are more qualified because they’re searching for your exact area.

Broad vs. Focused: The Numbers

Strategy Broad Targeting Focused Targeting
Target keyword “Phoenix real estate agent” “Chandler AZ real estate agent”
Competition level Extreme — 5,000+ agents Moderate — 200-400 agents
Proximity match Poor — too far from most searchers Strong — close to local searchers
Lead quality Low — random metro-wide clicks High — people in your exact market
Ranking difficulty Near impossible for new profiles Achievable within 90-180 days

The agents who generate consistent leads from Google made a deliberate choice: they picked one city, made it the center of their entire online presence, and built authority there before expanding.

How to Focus Your Targeting

  1. Choose your primary city

    This should be the city where your office is physically located. If you work from home, it’s the city where you live. Every optimization decision flows from this choice.

  2. Saturate your profile with that city name

    Your GBP description, posts, services, Q&A, and review responses should all mention your primary city repeatedly and naturally. Google needs dozens of consistent signals pointing to the same location.

  3. Build local content on your website

    Create neighborhood guides, market reports, school district overviews, and local event roundups for your primary city. This content builds topical authority and reinforces your geographic relevance.

  4. Expand only after you dominate

    Once you consistently rank in the top 3 for your primary city, then consider expanding to adjacent neighborhoods. Build a separate landing page for each new area and earn reviews from clients in those locations.

Agents who focus on a single city see 3-5x more leads from Google than agents who target an entire metro area with the same amount of effort. Specificity beats reach in local SEO.

Based on local SEO case studies from BrightLocal

Your Google Ranking Action Plan

Now you know the three factors. Here’s exactly what to do this week to start moving up in local search results.

Week 1: Fix Your Foundation (Relevance)

  1. Audit your Google Business Profile

    Log into your GBP and check: Is every field filled out? Do you have multiple categories? Are all your services listed? Is your description keyword-rich with your city name? If any answer is no, fix it today.

  2. Add 10+ services to your profile

    Go to the Services section and add every service you offer. Buyer representation, seller representation, market analysis, relocation, investment properties — each service is a keyword Google can match to searches.

  3. Rewrite your business description

    Include your full name, primary city, top 3 services, and years of experience. Make it 500-750 characters. Natural language, not keyword spam.

Week 2: Build Your Activity Engine

  1. Schedule your first 3 Google Posts

    Write a market update, a homebuyer tip, and a community spotlight. Include your city name in each one. Set a recurring calendar reminder to post 2-3 times per week going forward.

  2. Respond to every existing review

    Go back through all your reviews — yes, all of them — and write a keyword-optimized response for each one. Include your name, city, and the service you provided. This is retroactive SEO gold.

  3. Set up a review request system

    Create a template text message with your direct Google review link. Send it to every client within 24 hours of closing. Aim for 2-4 new reviews per month minimum.

Week 3-4: Lock In Your Location Focus

  1. Create a city-specific landing page

    Build one dedicated page on your website for your primary city. Include market stats, neighborhood highlights, and your local expertise. Link to it from your GBP.

  2. Upload 10 fresh photos

    Add recent photos of listings, local landmarks, your office, community events, and client meetings (with permission). Profiles with 100+ photos get 520% more calls than average.

The agents who rank on Google didn’t get lucky. They got specific about their city. They filled out their profile completely. And they showed up consistently, week after week, with posts, reviews, and fresh content. That’s the formula. No shortcuts, no hacks — just the three factors, executed with discipline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does proximity matter more than reviews for Google ranking?Google’s primary goal is to show the most useful result for the searcher. When someone searches “real estate agent near me,” Google interprets “near me” literally. A mediocre profile 1 mile away will often outrank an excellent profile 15 miles away because Google prioritizes geographic relevance for local searches. Reviews help within your proximity radius, but they can’t overcome significant distance.
How long does it take to start ranking on Google as a real estate agent?If you fully optimize your profile (relevance), maintain consistent activity (2-3 posts per week, review responses), and focus on a specific city, most agents see measurable improvement within 60-90 days. Ranking in the top 3 for your primary city typically takes 90-180 days of consistent effort. Agents targeting broad metro areas may never rank without narrowing their focus.
Can I rank in multiple cities on Google?Yes, but not at first. Google limits each Google Business Profile to one verified address. You will rank strongest in the city where your office is located. To rank in additional cities, build location-specific pages on your website, earn reviews from clients in those cities, and create content that mentions those areas. Some agents with multiple physical offices can create separate GBP listings for each location.
Do Google Posts actually help with search rankings?Yes. Google Posts are indexed content that signals to Google that your profile is active and relevant. Posts that include your target keywords and city name provide direct relevance signals. While a single post won’t dramatically change your ranking, consistent weekly posting is one of the strongest activity signals Google measures. Agents who post 2-3 times per week consistently outrank those who post sporadically.
What should I do if I already have a Google Business Profile but get no leads?Start with an audit. Check if your profile is 100% complete: multiple categories, all services listed, keyword-rich description, recent photos, and active posts. Then check your review responses — are they keyword-optimized with your name, city, and services? Finally, evaluate your targeting. If you are trying to rank for an entire metro area, narrow your focus to your specific city. Most agents who get zero leads from Google have an incomplete profile, no activity in the last 90 days, or are targeting too broad a geography.
