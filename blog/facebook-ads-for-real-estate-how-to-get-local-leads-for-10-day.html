Facebook Ads for Real Estate: How to Get Local Leads for $10/Day

TRENT STONEHOUSE16 MIN READMARCH 31, 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

Facebook ads remain one of the most cost-effective ways for real estate agents to generate local leads in 2026. With a $10/day budget, proper local targeting (15-mile radius around your farm area), and the right campaign structure (awareness + lead gen + retargeting), agents can generate 5–15 qualified leads per week at $3–$8 per lead. The key is running three campaigns simultaneously, using Meta’s lead forms for mobile users, and pairing paid ads with consistent organic content to build trust before the click.

Every real estate coach says you should run Facebook ads. Most agents try it, burn $200 in a week, get zero closings, and decide it doesn’t work. That’s not a Facebook problem. That’s a setup problem.

The agents who consistently close deals from Facebook ads are not spending thousands per month. They are spending $10/day with surgical targeting, a three-campaign funnel, and follow-up systems that convert leads into appointments. This guide breaks down exactly how they do it.

Facebook reaches 73% of U.S. adults aged 25–54 — the core home-buying demographic. The average cost per lead for real estate on Meta platforms is $4–$12, compared to $30–$50+ on Google Ads and $20–$40 on Zillow Premier Agent.

Sources: Pew Research, Social Media Use 2025 | WordStream, Facebook Ads Benchmarks

Why Facebook Ads Still Work for Real Estate in 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

Facebook ads work for real estate because the platform combines hyper-local targeting, low cost per lead, and massive reach in the exact demographics that buy and sell homes. No other paid channel lets you target homeowners within a 10-mile radius of a specific zip code for under $10/day.

There’s a persistent myth that Facebook is dead for advertising. It’s not. Meta’s ad platform (which includes Facebook and Instagram) still reaches more U.S. adults than any other social network. And for real estate specifically, it has three advantages that no competitor matches.

Advantage 1: Hyper-Local Targeting

Real estate is local. You don’t need to reach everyone in your state. You need to reach homeowners and potential buyers within a specific radius of your farm area. Facebook lets you draw a pin on a map and target everyone within 10–25 miles. Layer on age, income, homeownership status, and interests — and you’re reaching exactly the right people.

Advantage 2: Cost Efficiency

The average real estate lead on Facebook costs $4–$12. Compare that to Zillow Premier Agent ($20–$50 per lead), Google Ads ($30–$50 per lead), or buying a lead list ($15–$25 per unverified contact). Facebook gives you more leads for less money — especially if you pair it with organic content that warms up your audience before they see the ad.

Advantage 3: Visual-First Format

Real estate sells on visuals. Homes, neighborhoods, lifestyle. Facebook and Instagram are built for image and video content. A well-shot listing walkthrough or a carousel of neighborhood highlights performs better here than on any text-based platform.

Meta Ads Manager vs. the Boost Button

The “Boost Post” button on your Facebook page is not an ad strategy. It gives you limited targeting, no conversion tracking, and no retargeting capability. Every ad in this guide runs through Meta Ads Manager (ads.facebook.com), which gives you full control over audience, placement, budget, and optimization. If you’ve only ever boosted posts, you have not actually run Facebook ads.

The $10/Day Budget Breakdown: What to Expect

⚡ Quick Answer

Split your $10/day across three campaigns: $3/day on awareness (video views), $5/day on lead generation (lead forms), and $2/day on retargeting (warm audience follow-up). At $300/month, expect 25–60 leads, 5–10 conversations, and 1–2 appointments from this budget alone.

A $10/day budget is $300/month. That’s less than the cost of one closing gift. But it only works if you split that budget strategically across multiple campaign types instead of dumping it all into one ad.

The Three-Campaign Split

  1. Awareness Campaign — $3/day ($90/month)

    Objective: Video Views. Run a 30–60 second video introducing yourself, showcasing a neighborhood, or sharing a market update. The goal is not leads — it’s recognition. You’re building a warm audience of people who have watched your content. This audience becomes your retargeting pool.

  2. Lead Generation Campaign — $5/day ($150/month)

    Objective: Leads. Use Meta’s instant lead forms (not a landing page — more on that later). Offer something valuable: a free home valuation, a list of homes under $400K in the area, or a first-time buyer checklist. This is where your leads come from. Expect $4–$10 per lead at this budget level.

  3. Retargeting Campaign — $2/day ($60/month)

    Objective: Leads or Traffic. Show ads only to people who watched your awareness videos, visited your website, or engaged with your page. These people already know you. Retargeting leads cost 40–60% less than cold leads because the trust is already built.

The average real estate commission on a $350K home is $10,500. At $300/month in ad spend, you need one closing every 3 months just to break even — and most agents close their first Facebook-sourced deal within 60–90 days of running a proper funnel.

Campaign Structure: Awareness vs. Lead Gen vs. Retargeting

⚡ Quick Answer

A complete Facebook ad funnel for real estate uses three campaign types working together: awareness (introduces you to cold audiences), lead generation (captures contact info from interested prospects), and retargeting (follows up with people who already engaged). Skipping any one of these creates a leaky funnel.

Most agents run one campaign and wonder why it doesn’t work. A single lead gen ad to a cold audience is like proposing on a first date. You need to warm people up before you ask for their information.

Stage 1: Awareness (Top of Funnel)

The awareness campaign is your introduction. You are not asking for anything. You are showing up, providing value, and letting people get to know you. The best awareness ads are:

  • Market update videos — 30–60 seconds of local data, trends, what’s happening in your area
  • Neighborhood tours — Walk through a local community and highlight what makes it special
  • Tips and education — First-time buyer mistakes, what to look for in a home inspection, how to improve your credit score

Set the campaign objective to Video Views or Engagement. Target your local area (15–25 mile radius). Let this run continuously — it’s building your warm audience every day.

Stage 2: Lead Generation (Middle of Funnel)

Once someone has watched your videos or engaged with your page, they are warm. Now you can run a lead generation campaign that targets both cold and warm audiences with an offer:

  • Free home valuation — “What’s your home worth in today’s market? Get a free report.”
  • Curated property list — “See all homes under $400K in [City] before they hit the MLS.”
  • Buyer/seller guide — “Download our free 2026 Home Buyer Checklist.”

Stage 3: Retargeting (Bottom of Funnel)

Retargeting ads show only to people who have already interacted with you — video viewers, website visitors, page engagers, or people who opened but didn’t submit a lead form. These are your warmest prospects.

“The money is in the follow-up. Your first ad gets attention. Your retargeting ad gets the lead. Your follow-up call gets the appointment.”

Retargeting ads should feel different from your cold ads. Use testimonials, social proof, and urgency. “37 families helped this year. Ready to be next?” or “See why 50+ clients gave us 5 stars on Google.”

Targeting That Reaches Your Ideal Clients

⚡ Quick Answer

The best Facebook ad targeting for real estate combines location radius (10–25 miles around your farm area), age range (28–65), homeownership status, and behavioral signals like “likely to move” or “recently engaged.” Once you have 1,000+ leads or website visitors, build a lookalike audience to find people who resemble your best clients.

Targeting is where most agents go wrong. They either go too broad (“everyone in Texas”) or too narrow (“only people who liked Zillow and are aged 32”). The sweet spot is a warm, local, behavior-based audience.

The Core Targeting Stack

  1. Location — Pin Drop + Radius

    Drop a pin on your farm area and set a 10–25 mile radius. For urban markets, 10–15 miles. For suburban or rural, 20–25 miles. Select “People living in this location” (not “recently in this location” which includes tourists and commuters).

  2. Age — 28 to 65

    This covers first-time buyers through downsizers. If you specialize in a niche (first-time buyers, luxury, seniors), narrow accordingly. But for a general real estate campaign, 28–65 gives Meta enough room to optimize.

  3. Detailed Targeting — Behaviors and Interests

    Under “Detailed Targeting,” look for behaviors like “Likely to move,” “Homeowners,” “Renters,” or life events like “Recently engaged” or “Newly married.” Note: Meta has removed some housing-specific targeting under Special Ad Category rules — you cannot target by zip code, age under 18, or certain demographics in housing ads.

  4. Custom Audiences — Your Warm Lists

    Upload your CRM contact list, past client emails, or sphere of influence. Create custom audiences from website visitors (via Meta Pixel), video viewers (anyone who watched 50%+ of your awareness videos), and page/post engagers. These are your retargeting gold.

  5. Lookalike Audiences — Scale What Works

    Once you have 1,000+ people in a custom audience (past clients, leads, or converters), create a 1% lookalike audience. Meta will find people who statistically resemble your best clients. This is the highest-performing cold audience you can build.

Special Ad Category: What You Need to Know

All housing-related ads on Meta must be filed under the Special Ad Category for Housing. This means you cannot target by zip code (must use a 15-mile minimum radius), cannot exclude by age/gender/ethnicity, and cannot use certain interest categories. Work within these rules — agents who comply still generate excellent results with behavioral and lookalike targeting.

Ad Creative That Actually Converts

⚡ Quick Answer

The three highest-converting ad formats for real estate are video walkthroughs (for awareness), carousel ads (for showcasing multiple listings or tips), and single-image ads with a clear offer (for lead generation). Every ad needs a scroll-stopping hook in the first 3 seconds, a clear value proposition, and a direct call to action.

Your ad creative is what makes someone stop scrolling. It does not matter how good your targeting is if your ad looks like every other generic real estate post. Here is what actually works.

Format 1: Video Ads (Best for Awareness)

  • Length: 30–60 seconds. Hook in the first 3 seconds.
  • Style: Talk directly to the camera, or do a walkthrough/tour.
  • Captions: Always on. 85% of Facebook video is watched without sound.
  • Hook examples: “The housing market in [City] just shifted — here’s what it means for you.” or “3 things every first-time buyer in [City] needs to know right now.”

Format 2: Carousel Ads (Best for Lead Gen)

  • Use: Show 3–5 listings, neighborhood highlights, or a step-by-step process.
  • First card: The hook. A bold statement or question. Not a listing photo.
  • Last card: The CTA. “See all available homes — tap to get the list.”
  • Design: Clean, professional, branded. Use your colors and headshot on every card.

Format 3: Single Image Ads (Best for Retargeting)

  • Use: A clear, compelling offer with minimal text.
  • Image: A professional photo of you, a stunning home, or a local landmark.
  • Text overlay: Keep it under 20% of the image. Lead with the offer, not your name.
  • Example: Image of a beautiful home + text: “Homes under $400K in [City]. Get the list before they’re gone.”

Video ads generate 2x more clicks and 20–30% lower cost per lead compared to static image ads in real estate campaigns. The combination of face-on-camera trust and visual property content outperforms every other format.

Source: Social Media Examiner, Facebook Video Ads Report

The Ad Copy Formula

Every high-converting real estate ad follows this structure:

  1. Hook — A question or bold statement that stops the scroll. “Thinking about selling your home in [City]?”
  2. Pain/desire — Address what they’re feeling. “You deserve to know what your home is actually worth — not a Zestimate.”
  3. Offer — What they get. “Get a free, no-obligation home valuation from a local expert.”
  4. CTA — What to do next. “Tap below to get your free report in 24 hours.”

Landing Page vs. Lead Form: When to Use Each

⚡ Quick Answer

Use Meta’s instant lead forms for most real estate campaigns — they auto-fill user info, keep people on Facebook, and convert at 2–3x the rate of landing pages on mobile. Use a landing page only when you need to pre-qualify leads with detailed questions or showcase a specific property with photos and details.

This is one of the most common debates in real estate advertising. The answer depends on your goal and your follow-up speed.

Factor Landing Page Meta Lead Form
Conversion rate 5–15% (requires page load) 15–30% (auto-fills info)
Lead quality Higher — more friction filters tire-kickers Lower — easy submission means more casual leads
Mobile experience Varies — depends on page speed Seamless — native Facebook experience
Best for Luxury listings, detailed property showcases Home valuations, buyer lists, general lead gen
Follow-up requirement Within 1 hour Within 5 minutes (leads go cold fast)

The recommendation for most agents: Start with Meta lead forms. They are faster to set up, convert at higher rates, and cost less per lead. But — and this is critical — you must follow up within 5 minutes. Lead form leads are low-friction, which means the prospect barely remembers filling it out. If you wait 24 hours, that lead is dead.

“Speed to lead is everything. The agent who calls within 5 minutes wins the appointment 78% of the time. The agent who waits an hour wins it 36% of the time. After 24 hours, you’re basically cold-calling.”
— Inside Sales Research, Lead Response Study

Pro Tip: Add Qualifying Questions to Lead Forms

To improve lead quality on Meta lead forms, add 1–2 custom questions: “Are you looking to buy or sell?” and “What’s your timeline?” This small amount of friction filters out the least serious prospects while keeping conversion rates high. You can also set the form to “Higher Intent” mode, which adds a review screen before submission.

Tracking, Optimization, and Scaling Your Ads

⚡ Quick Answer

Install the Meta Pixel on your website, set up Conversions API for accurate tracking, and monitor three metrics weekly: cost per lead, lead-to-appointment rate, and cost per appointment. Let campaigns run for at least 5–7 days before making changes. When a campaign is profitable, scale by increasing budget 20% every 3–5 days — never double it overnight.

Running ads without tracking is like showing homes without asking what the buyer wants. You are spending money blind. Here is the minimum tracking setup every agent needs.

Essential Tracking Setup

  1. Install the Meta Pixel

    The Meta Pixel is a snippet of code on your website that tracks visitors, conversions, and page views. It also builds your retargeting audience automatically. If you have a WordPress site, use a plugin like PixelYourSite to install it in 5 minutes.

  2. Set Up Conversions API

    Browser-based tracking (the Pixel) is becoming less reliable due to iOS privacy updates and ad blockers. Conversions API sends data server-side, ensuring Meta gets accurate conversion data. Most CRMs and website builders support this natively now.

  3. Connect Your CRM

    Leads from Meta lead forms should flow directly into your CRM (Follow Up Boss, KV Core, LionDesk, etc.) via Zapier or a native integration. This enables instant notification and automated follow-up sequences. Never manually check Ads Manager for new leads.

  4. Track the Right Metrics

    Forget impressions and reach. The three numbers that matter: Cost per lead (target: under $10), Lead-to-appointment rate (target: 10–20%), and Cost per appointment (target: under $75). Track these weekly in a simple spreadsheet.

Optimization Rules

  • Wait 5–7 days before judging a new campaign. Meta needs time to exit the “learning phase.”
  • Test one variable at a time. Change the image OR the headline OR the audience — never all three.
  • Kill underperformers fast. If a campaign has spent 3x your target cost per lead with zero conversions, pause it.
  • Scale winners slowly. Increase budget by 20% every 3–5 days. Doubling overnight resets the algorithm.
  • Refresh creative every 2–4 weeks. Even great ads experience creative fatigue. Swap images and headlines to keep performance steady.

Agents who refresh their ad creative every 2–3 weeks see 25–40% lower cost per lead compared to those who let the same ad run for months. Creative fatigue is the #1 reason ad performance declines over time.

Source: Meta, Ad Delivery and Performance Best Practices

Common Facebook Ad Mistakes That Waste Your Budget

⚡ Quick Answer

The five biggest budget-killers are: boosting posts instead of using Ads Manager, targeting too broadly, running only one campaign type, no follow-up system for leads, and ignoring organic content. Fix these five and your ad ROI will improve immediately.

Mistake #1: Boosting Posts Instead of Running Ads

The Boost button is Meta’s way of getting you to spend money with minimal control. You get basic engagement targeting, no conversion optimization, and no retargeting capability. Every dollar spent boosting is a dollar that could perform 3–5x better in Ads Manager. Delete the habit.

Mistake #2: Targeting Too Broadly

Running ads to “everyone aged 18–65 in my state” is throwing money away. Your ad will be shown to thousands of people who will never buy or sell a home in your area. Narrow to a 10–25 mile radius, layer in behavioral targeting, and let the algorithm optimize from there.

Mistake #3: Running Only Lead Gen With No Warm-Up

Asking cold strangers for their phone number is the advertising equivalent of cold-calling. Run an awareness campaign first. Build a warm audience. Then run lead gen to people who already recognize your face and name. The funnel approach costs less per lead and produces higher quality leads.

Mistake #4: No Follow-Up System

Generating 30 leads a month means nothing if you don’t call them. Facebook leads are not like referrals — they do not have built-in trust. You need to call within 5 minutes, send a text, and have an automated email drip. Without follow-up, you are paying for names and phone numbers that go nowhere.

Mistake #5: Running Ads Without Organic Content

When someone sees your ad and clicks on your profile, what do they find? If the answer is “a dead page with no recent posts,” they will not submit a lead form. Your organic content is the trust layer that makes your ads work. This is where consistent posting through a service like VEXI (formerly Social Realtr) pays for itself — your ads drive people to a profile full of professional, recent, relevant content that builds credibility instantly.

The Organic + Paid Flywheel

The most effective real estate advertising strategy pairs consistent organic content (posted daily across multiple platforms) with targeted paid ads. Your organic content builds trust, authority, and an engaged following. Your paid ads amplify that content and capture leads at scale.

“Paid ads get you in front of people. Organic content is what makes them trust you enough to respond. You need both.”
