YouTube growth tricks that work right now, for Shorts and long-form

Titles and thumbnails you can test, the end-screen loop, chapters, cadence, the Shorts-to-long-form funnel and posts. Then the gray-area tricks people use, and what each one can cost your channel.

IN SHORT

  • The tricks that hold up are the ones YouTube built tools for: Test & Compare, end screens, chapters, the related-video link on Shorts, and posts.
  • YouTube says it judges each video on its own and does not punish breaks, so steady quality beats a forced daily schedule.
  • Deleting and re-uploading, buying views and paying for subscribers can get a channel flagged or terminated. The rules are linked below.

Most YouTube growth advice is folklore. Some of it works. Some of it quietly hurts your channel. This guide sticks to what YouTube itself says, and it links the page each time.

One fact frames everything below. YouTube says its discovery system is focused more on individual videos than on channel averages. It also says recommendations are pulled by each viewer, not pushed out to everyone. So every trick here is about one thing: making each video easier to click, easier to keep watching, and easier to follow to the next one.

Titles and thumbnails: test them, do not guess

The title and thumbnail decide whether anyone clicks. You no longer have to guess which one works. YouTube Studio's title and thumbnail testing lets you try up to three titles, three thumbnails, or both together on one long-form video.

Here is the part people miss. The winner is picked by watch time, not clicks. A thumbnail that gets more clicks but makes people leave early can lose. That is the right test, because a click that bounces does not help you.

The limits: it runs on desktop only, needs advanced features turned on, and does not work on Shorts. Changing the title or thumbnail during a test stops it.

The honest risk: none, as long as the video delivers what the thumbnail promises. YouTube's spam and deceptive practices policy bans titles, thumbnails or descriptions that trick people into watching something the video does not contain. Bold is fine. A lie is not.

The end-screen and playlist loop

The cheapest view you will ever get is the next one from someone already watching. End screens let you place a video, a playlist, a subscribe button or a link in the last 5 to 20 seconds of a long video.

The trick is to build a loop. Group your videos into playlists by topic. End every video by pointing at the playlist it belongs to, or at the one video that naturally comes next. Say it out loud too: “If this helped, the next one covers…” A spoken line plus an end screen beats an end screen alone.

Two limits from the same Help page. End screens do not show on videos set as made for kids, and viewers can hide them. So do not rely on them alone. Put the next video in the description and in a pinned comment too.

Chapters that people actually use

Chapters break a long video into named parts. Viewers jump to the part they want instead of leaving. YouTube's chapter rules are short: the first timestamp must be 00:00, you need at least three, they go in order, and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long.

Write chapter names the way people search: “How much does a roof repair cost” beats “Part 2”. If you skip it, YouTube may add automatic chapters, but not every video gets them. Manual chapters always win over automatic ones.

The Shorts to long-form funnel

Shorts can now run up to three minutes if they are square or vertical. That leaves room to tell a small story, then point at the long version.

The tool for that is the related video link. In YouTube Studio you can attach one related video from your own channel to any Short. It shows as a tappable link on the Short. The trick: cut the Short from the long video, end it on an open question, and link the long video that answers it.

Will Shorts viewers drag down your long videos? YouTube says no. Its creator liaison wrote that homepage recommendations mostly use data from the same traffic source, so a Shorts audience does not weigh on how long-form is recommended.

Posting cadence: steady beats daily

“Post every day or the algorithm forgets you” is folklore. YouTube's own growth team has said there is no penalty box for taking breaks or for a dip in views, and that one weak video does not sink a channel.

So pick the pace you can keep at your best quality. For most small businesses that is one long video a week or every two weeks, plus a few Shorts cut from it. Old videos can also come back. YouTube says recommendations are not limited to new uploads, so a video that answers a real question can keep earning views for years.

Posts: the tab most channels ignore

YouTube lets channels publish posts: text, images, polls and quizzes that show on your channel and can appear in subscribers' feeds. Eligibility has changed over the years, so check the Create menu in your own Studio.

Use posts between uploads. Ask a poll question that your next video answers. Share a behind-the-scenes photo the day before a release. It keeps your channel in front of subscribers without a new upload.

The honest risk: keep polls honest. YouTube's spam policy covers polls and image posts used to pressure people into engagement.

The gray-area tricks, and their honest risk

These are the tricks people trade in creator chats. Some are fine. Some are against the rules. Here is each one, plainly.

Pinning your own comment. Fine. YouTube lets you pin one comment to the top of each video, yours or a viewer's. Pin a question that starts a thread, or a link to the next video. Gray turns black when the “fan” comment you pin was written by you on a second account. That is fake engagement, and it is the kind of deception YouTube's fake engagement policy is written for.

Deleting a Short and re-uploading it to “test” it again. Risky. YouTube's own creator liaison wrote that re-uploading a weak video might be treated as spam, and your channel could be flagged. You also lose every view and comment the first upload earned. Make a new, better version instead. If you want a real test, use title and thumbnail testing on long-form.

Posting the same video over and over with small changes. Against the rules. The spam policy bans using automated tools or AI to churn out lots of near-identical videos.

Re-uploading other people's clips. It loses reach now. In October 2026 YouTube said it cut Shorts reach for channels that re-upload other creators' work with minimal changes.

“Sub for sub” and rewards for subscribers. Against the rules. Asking people to like and subscribe is allowed. Offering a reward in exchange for likes, views or subscribers is spam under the same policy.

Buying views or subscribers. Against the rules, and in the US it can be against the law. YouTube removes fake engagement and may terminate channels built on it. The FTC's 2024 rule also bans buying or selling fake indicators of social media influence, like bought followers or views, to look bigger for business.

What happens when you cross the line: YouTube can remove a video and give a warning or a strike. Under its spam policy, three strikes in 90 days can end the channel.

Questions, answered.

Does posting every day help on YouTube?

Not by itself. YouTube says it judges each video on its own and does not punish breaks or a dip in views. Post at the pace you can keep at your best quality.

Is it OK to delete a Short and upload it again?

It is risky. YouTube has said re-uploading a weak video might be treated as spam, and you lose the first upload’s views and comments. Make a new, better version instead.

Can I A/B test my YouTube thumbnails?

Yes, on long-form videos. YouTube Studio lets you test up to three titles, thumbnails or both, on desktop, and picks the winner by watch time. It does not work on Shorts.

Is buying YouTube views illegal?

It breaks YouTube’s fake engagement policy, which can end a channel. In the US, the FTC’s 2024 rule also bans buying or selling fake indicators of social media influence to look bigger for business.
