Cold Calling vs Warm Calling: The Right Way to Use the Phone in Real Estate

TRENT STONEHOUSE4 MIN READAPRIL 1, 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

Real estate phone prospecting works best when you prioritize warm calling over cold calling. Warm calls — reaching out to past clients, sphere, and known contacts — convert at dramatically higher rates because trust already exists. Cold calling still works for FSBOs and expireds, but the real money is in reconnecting with people who already know your name. The phone is a blue ocean: most agents avoid it, making it one of the most powerful tools available.

Most agents will never pick up the phone. They’ll spend hours on social media, agonize over email subject lines, and shoot a dozen takes for a Reel. But a 60-second phone call? That’s where they freeze. And that’s exactly why real estate phone prospecting is one of the most powerful competitive advantages you can build in 2026.

The phone is a blue ocean. While everyone’s drowning in a red ocean of content, ads, and DMs, the agents who make calls are having real conversations that lead to real closings.

“If you’re not using the phone to grow your business, you’re choosing to stay small. That might sound harsh. But it’s true.”
— Trent Stonehouse, Your One Conversation Away…

Cold Calling vs Warm Calling — What’s the Difference?

Cold Calling vs Warm Calling

Cold calling means reaching out to people who don’t know you: FSBOs, expired listings, random homeowners. Warm calling means reconnecting with people who already know your name: past clients, sphere of influence, event attendees, and referral partners. Both have a place in real estate phone prospecting, but warm calls convert at dramatically higher rates.

Here’s why most agents hate cold calling: they’re calling strangers with no context, no trust, and no relationship. It feels awkward because it is awkward. But warm calling? That’s reaching out to someone who already knows you, likes you, and just needs a reminder that you’re still in the game.

The probability of converting a past client is 60–70%. New cold leads? Just 5–20%. Most agents overspend chasing strangers while leaving easy wins with warm contacts untouched.

Source: Marketing Metrics: The Definitive Guide

Why Most Agents Don’t Call — And What It’s Costing Them

Let’s break down the excuses. Most agents don’t make calls because it “feels awkward,” they “don’t want to bother anyone,” they “don’t know what to say,” they “sound like a salesperson,” or they “don’t want rejection.” Sound familiar?

But here’s the irony. You’ll spend hours tweaking your social posts. Agonize over email subject lines. Shoot a dozen takes for a Reel. But a 60-second phone call could start a conversation that turns into a listing, a referral, or a $15,000 commission. Real estate phone prospecting isn’t about cold-calling the phone book. It’s about connecting with past clients, sphere, warm leads, and future deals — on purpose.

Build a Warm Calling System That Runs on Autopilot

Here’s the real estate phone prospecting system that top producers use:

  1. Segment Your Database

    Past clients, sphere of influence, warm leads, referral partners. Each group gets a different call script and frequency.

  2. Hire a VA to Do the Dialing

    Sites like OnlineJobs.ph have Filipino professionals who make 100–140+ calls per day for $5/hour. They book coffees, reconnect with past clients, and set qualified appointments.

  3. Lead with Value, Not a Pitch

    Ask how their family is. Mention a local event. Offer coffee. Share neighborhood insights. When you show up as the helpful local pro — not the desperate cold-caller — people welcome the call.

  4. Track and Follow Up

    Log every call in your CRM. Set follow-up reminders. The fortune is in the follow-up — most deals happen after the 5th–12th touchpoint.

The Simple Math: More Calls = More Closings

Real estate phone prospecting isn’t magic. It’s math, multiplied by effort.

  • Call 15 past clients → Get a lunch, a referral, or a listing
  • Call 15 warm leads → Get a showing or a consult
  • Call 15 new contacts a day → 300+ conversations a month

Alex Hormozi shared a story about a client who was failing with Facebook ads but crushing it by knocking doors. For every 15 homes he knocked, he got a lead. Same logic applies to real estate phone prospecting: the effort-to-result ratio is clear, predictable, and scalable.

“While most of the industry is waiting for leads to hit their inbox, you’ll be creating real momentum. And listings? They follow the conversations.”

Frequently Asked Questions

Is cold calling dead in real estate?No. Cold calling is a blue ocean strategy — most agents avoid it, which makes it more powerful for those who do it. However, warm calling (past clients, sphere, known contacts) converts at 3–5x higher rates and should be the priority in any real estate phone prospecting system.
What is the best phone script for real estate prospecting?Lead with value, not a pitch. “Hi, this is [Name] calling on behalf of [Agent], your local Realtor in [City]. We’re setting up client coffee meetups and wanted to reconnect.” The goal is conversation, not conversion. Offers of coffee, lunch, or local event info work best.
How many calls should a real estate agent make per day?Aim for a minimum of 15 connected calls per day. A VA can make 100–140+ dials daily, connecting with 15–25 people. Consistency matters more than volume — 15 calls per day for 30 days is 450 conversations per month.
Should I hire a VA or do my own calling?Both. Handle your warm sphere personally for maximum impact, and hire a VA for real estate phone prospecting at scale. A VA at $5/hour making 140 dials per day is one of the highest-ROI investments in real estate.
