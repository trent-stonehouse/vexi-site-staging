How to Get Into Google’s Local 3-Pack as a Real Estate Agent

TRENT STONEHOUSE13 MIN READMARCH 31, 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

To get into Google’s local 3-pack as a real estate agent, you need to fully optimize your Google Business Profile (name, categories, services), collect 25+ reviews containing location and service keywords, and post to your profile weekly. The local 3-pack captures 79% of clicks in local searches. Google ranks you based on three factors: relevance, distance, and prominence. Most agents lose because their profile is incomplete — not because the competition is better.

When someone searches “real estate agent near me” or “homes for sale in [your city],” Google shows a map with three businesses underneath it. That’s the local 3-pack. If you’re not in those three spots, you’re invisible to the majority of local searchers.

This guide breaks down every step to get your Google Business Profile ranked in the 3-pack — from profile optimization and keyword-loaded reviews to weekly posting strategies. It combines the tactics from two proven frameworks: the Google algorithm cheat sheet and the 60-second ranking checklist.

79% of people who do a local search on their phone visit a business within 24 hours. And 92% of searchers pick a business on the first page of local results. If you’re not in the 3-pack, you’re not on the first page.

Sources: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey, 2025 | Google Business Profile

What Is Google’s Local 3-Pack (And Why It Gets 79% of Clicks)

The local 3-pack is the box of three business listings that appears below the map in Google search results for local queries. It shows the business name, star rating, number of reviews, address, hours, and a link to the website. For real estate searches, it is the single most valuable piece of digital real estate you can own.

Here’s why it matters so much for real estate agents:

  • 79% of local search clicks go to the 3-pack results, not the organic links below them
  • 76% of people who search for something nearby visit a related business within one day
  • The 3-pack appears above organic results — even above websites that have spent years building SEO authority
  • Mobile searches dominate — and the 3-pack takes up nearly the entire screen on a phone

Google Business Profile vs. Google My Business

Google renamed “Google My Business” to “Google Business Profile” (GBP) in 2022. They are the same thing. If you hear either term, it refers to the free listing you manage at business.google.com. Your GBP is the listing that appears in the 3-pack.

Think of it this way: when a homeowner in your city types “best real estate agent [city name]” into Google, the 3-pack is the first thing they see. If your name is there with 50+ five-star reviews, that homeowner is calling you — not scrolling down to page two.

The agents who dominate the 3-pack are not necessarily the best agents. They are the ones who optimize their Google Business Profile correctly. And most agents don’t — which means the opportunity is wide open.

The 3 Factors That Determine Your 3-Pack Ranking

Google uses three factors to decide which businesses appear in the local 3-pack: relevance, distance, and prominence. Understanding these three factors is the foundation of every optimization tactic in this guide.

1. Relevance

Relevance measures how well your Google Business Profile matches what the searcher is looking for. If someone searches “listing agent in Austin TX” and your profile says “real estate agent” with Austin as your service area and “listing agent” in your services — you’re relevant.

You control relevance through:

  • Your business name and primary category
  • Your services and service descriptions
  • Keywords in your reviews
  • Content in your Google Business Profile posts

2. Distance

Distance is how far your business location is from the searcher. You can’t fake this. If someone searches from the north side of town and your office is on the south side, a closer competitor has an edge.

What you can do: make sure your address is accurate, your service area is correctly defined, and your NAP (Name, Address, Phone) is consistent across every directory and citation online.

3. Prominence

Prominence is how well-known and trusted Google considers your business. This is the factor you have the most control over. Google measures prominence through:

  • Review count and quality — more reviews with higher ratings = more prominent
  • Review velocity — getting reviews consistently over time, not 20 in one week then nothing
  • Online citations — your business listed on Zillow, Realtor.com, Yelp, BBB, and local directories
  • Website authority — backlinks, domain age, and organic traffic to your site
  • Google Business Profile activity — regular posts, photo uploads, and Q&A responses

Reviews are the #1 local ranking factor, accounting for approximately 17% of Google’s local pack ranking algorithm. Businesses with 25+ reviews earn significantly more clicks than those with fewer than 10.

Source: Moz Local Search Ranking Factors Study, 2024

The rest of this guide focuses on the specific actions you need to take to maximize relevance and prominence — the two factors you actually control.

Optimize Your Google Business Profile Name

Your Google Business Profile name should be your real business name — but make sure it includes location and service keywords where Google allows it. This is one of the strongest relevance signals in the local algorithm.

Here’s the difference between a weak profile name and a strong one:

Weak Profile Name Strong Profile Name
Jane Smith Jane Smith — Austin Real Estate Agent
Smith Realty Group Smith Realty Group — Homes for Sale in Austin TX
RE/MAX Elite RE/MAX Elite — Austin Listing & Buyer’s Agent

Important: Google’s guidelines say your name should reflect your real-world business name. Adding keywords that aren’t part of your registered business name can get your profile suspended. The safest approach is to register your DBA (Doing Business As) with your state to include your city and service type, then use that as your GBP name.

Complete Every Profile Field

A half-filled profile tells Google you’re not serious. Fill out every single field:

  • Primary category: “Real estate agent” (this is non-negotiable)
  • Secondary categories: “Real estate agency,” “Real estate consultant,” “Property management company” — add every relevant one
  • Business description: 750 characters packed with your city, neighborhoods, and services
  • Service area: Every city, zip code, and neighborhood you serve
  • Hours: Set accurate business hours (Google penalizes inconsistencies)
  • Phone number: Use a local area code, not a toll-free number
  • Website URL: Link to a location-specific landing page, not just your homepage

NAP Consistency

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone number. Your NAP must be identical — character for character — across your Google Business Profile, website, Zillow, Realtor.com, Facebook, and every other directory. Even small differences (“St.” vs. “Street”) can hurt your ranking. Use a tool like Moz Local to audit your citations.

Add Every Service You Offer (The Hidden Keyword Hack)

The “Services” section of your Google Business Profile is one of the most underused ranking tools available. Most agents either skip it entirely or add one generic service. That’s a massive missed opportunity.

Google uses your services list to determine relevance for specific search queries. When someone searches “luxury listing agent in [city]” and you have “Luxury Home Listing Services” as a service with a keyword-rich description — you match that query.

Services to Add to Your Profile

Add every service you legitimately provide. Each one is a keyword signal to Google:

  1. Buyer Representation

    “Full-service buyer representation for first-time homebuyers and investors in [city] and surrounding areas. Includes market analysis, home tours, and negotiation.”

  2. Home Listing Services

    “Professional home listing services including comparative market analysis, professional photography, staging consultation, and MLS listing for [city] homeowners.”

  3. Luxury Real Estate

    “Luxury home sales and purchases in [city]. Specializing in properties above $750K with premium marketing and private showings.”

  4. Relocation Services

    “Helping families and professionals relocate to [city]. Area tours, school district guidance, neighborhood comparisons, and remote buying support.”

  5. Investment Property Consulting

    “Real estate investment analysis for rental properties, flips, and multi-family units in the [city] metro area. ROI projections and market trend data.”

  6. Home Valuation / CMA

    “Free comparative market analysis for homeowners in [city]. Find out what your home is worth in today’s market.”

Pro tip: Replace “[city]” with your actual city name in every service description. Each description can be up to 300 characters. Use all of them. Every word is a keyword signal.

Also add additional services like: property management referrals, new construction, short sales, foreclosures, commercial real estate, land sales, and estate/probate sales — if you handle them. More services = more keyword matches = more relevance.

Get 25+ Reviews with the Right Keywords

Reviews are the single most influential factor for local 3-pack rankings. But it’s not just about star ratings — it’s about what your reviewers actually write. Google reads the text of every review and uses it as a relevance signal.

A review that says “Great agent!” does almost nothing for your ranking. A review that says “Jane helped us sell our home in North Austin in just 12 days. Best listing agent we’ve ever worked with” — that hits multiple ranking keywords: sell, home, North Austin, listing agent.

How to Get Keyword-Rich Reviews (Without Being Fake)

You don’t tell clients what to write. You guide them with specific questions that naturally produce keyword-rich responses:

  • “What neighborhood did we help you buy/sell in?” — produces location keywords
  • “What type of property was it?” — produces service keywords (condo, luxury home, investment property)
  • “What was the most helpful part of working with us?” — produces service-specific language
  • “Would you recommend us to other buyers/sellers in [city]?” — produces location + service keywords

Businesses with 25+ Google reviews earn 73% more clicks than those with fewer than 10 reviews. And businesses that respond to reviews are 1.7x more likely to be considered trustworthy by consumers.

Source: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey, 2025

The Review Request System

  1. Create Your Review Link

    Go to your Google Business Profile, click “Ask for reviews,” and copy the direct review link. Save it somewhere you can access instantly.

  2. Ask at the Emotional Peak

    The best time to ask is at the closing table, the day keys are handed over, or right after a successful negotiation. Emotion drives action.

  3. Send a Text with the Link + Prompts

    “It was amazing working with you! If you have 2 minutes, would you mind sharing your experience? Here are a couple of things that help: mention the neighborhood, what we helped you with, and anything that stood out. [Review Link]”

  4. Follow Up Once

    If they don’t leave a review within 3 days, send one friendly follow-up. After that, move on. Never pressure.

Target: at least 2–3 new reviews per month. Consistency matters more than volume. Google rewards steady review velocity over time, not a sudden burst followed by silence.

Respond to Every Review with SEO Keywords

Your review responses are indexed by Google and count as additional keyword signals for your profile. This is a free ranking boost that most agents completely ignore. Every response is an opportunity to add location names, service types, and neighborhood keywords to your profile.

The Keyword-Loaded Response Formula

Follow this structure for every review response:

  1. Thank them by name — personalization shows you care
  2. Reference the specific service — “Helping you sell your home in [neighborhood] was a great experience”
  3. Include your city/area — “We love working with homeowners in [city]”
  4. Add a forward-looking keyword — “If anyone in [city] needs a listing agent, we’re here to help”

Example Review Response (Keyword-Optimized)

Sample Response Template

“Thank you so much, [Name]! It was a pleasure helping you find your dream home in [Neighborhood], [City]. The [City] real estate market moves fast, and we’re glad we could make the buying process smooth for you. If any of your friends or family need a real estate agent in [City], we’d love to help. Congratulations again on your new home!”

Notice how that response naturally includes: the neighborhood, the city (twice), “real estate agent,” “buying process,” and “dream home.” All of those are search terms. All of them help your ranking.

Respond to negative reviews too. A professional, empathetic response to a negative review shows potential clients (and Google) that you take your business seriously. Never argue. Acknowledge, apologize, and offer to resolve offline.

Response Timing Matters

Google tracks how quickly you respond to reviews. Aim to respond within 24 hours. Set up Google Business Profile notifications on your phone so you see new reviews immediately. Fast responses signal to Google that your profile is active and well-managed.

Post Weekly to Your Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile posts are like mini blog posts that appear directly on your listing. They expire after 7 days, which means Google rewards profiles that post consistently. Weekly posting signals that your business is active, and it gives Google fresh content to index for keyword relevance.

Most agents don’t even know this feature exists. That’s your advantage.

What to Post on Your Google Business Profile

Rotate through these four post types weekly:

  1. Market Update Post

    Share a local housing stat. “[City] home prices rose 4.2% this quarter. Here’s what that means for sellers in [Neighborhood].” Include a photo of the neighborhood.

  2. New Listing / Just Sold Post

    “Just listed: 4-bed, 3-bath home in [Neighborhood], [City]. $425K. DM for details.” Include the listing photo. This is your strongest relevance signal.

  3. Local Event / Community Post

    Share a local event, farmer’s market, school update, or community news. This builds local relevance and shows Google you’re connected to the area.

  4. Tip / Educational Post

    “3 things every first-time homebuyer in [City] should know before making an offer.” Educational content builds authority and earns clicks.

Every post should include: your city name, a relevant keyword (listing agent, homebuyer, real estate), a photo, and a call-to-action button (“Learn More,” “Call Now,” or “Book Online”).

Google Business Profiles that post weekly get 2x more actions (calls, direction requests, website clicks) than profiles that post monthly or less. Posts also appear in Google Maps results, giving you additional visibility.

Source: Google Business Profile Help Documentation

The 60-Second Google Ranking Checklist

Here is a complete checklist you can run through in 60 seconds to audit your Google Business Profile. If you can’t check every box, that’s your to-do list. Each unchecked item is a ranking opportunity you’re leaving on the table.

Profile Completeness

  • Business name includes city + service keyword (registered as DBA)
  • Primary category is “Real estate agent”
  • 3+ secondary categories added (agency, consultant, etc.)
  • Business description uses all 750 characters with location keywords
  • Service area covers every city and neighborhood you serve
  • Phone number has a local area code
  • Website links to a location-specific page
  • Hours are accurate and up to date

Services & Keywords

  • 6+ services added with keyword-rich descriptions
  • Each service description includes city name
  • Services cover buyer, seller, luxury, investment, and relocation

Reviews & Responses

  • 25+ total reviews
  • 4.5+ star average rating
  • Getting 2–3 new reviews per month (consistent velocity)
  • 100% of reviews have a response
  • Responses include city, neighborhood, and service keywords
  • Responding within 24 hours of each new review

Activity & Freshness

  • Posting to GBP at least once per week
  • Posts include photos and city keywords
  • Uploading new photos monthly (office, team, listings, community)
  • Answering all Q&A questions on your profile

Citations & Consistency

  • NAP is identical across all directories (Google, Zillow, Realtor.com, Yelp, Facebook)
  • Listed on 20+ local and industry directories
  • No duplicate Google Business Profiles

The 80/20 Rule for the 3-Pack

If you’re starting from zero, focus on three things first: (1) complete your profile 100%, (2) get to 25 reviews, and (3) start posting weekly. Those three actions account for roughly 80% of your ranking potential. Everything else is refinement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to get into the Google local 3-pack?Most agents see measurable improvement within 4–8 weeks of fully optimizing their Google Business Profile. Getting into the 3-pack depends on your market’s competition level, but agents in mid-size cities with 25+ reviews and weekly posting typically rank within 2–3 months. Larger metros may take longer due to higher competition.
Can I rank in the 3-pack without a physical office?Yes. Google allows service-area businesses (SABs) to create a Google Business Profile without displaying a physical address. You set your service area by city or zip code instead. Many top-ranking real estate agents operate as SABs. The key is that your service area, reviews, and content still include strong location keywords.
How many Google reviews do I need to rank in the 3-pack?There is no magic number, but data from BrightLocal shows that businesses with 25+ reviews significantly outperform those with fewer than 10. In most real estate markets, 40–50 reviews with a 4.5+ average puts you in strong contention. Focus on steady velocity (2–3 new reviews per month) rather than a single burst.
Do Google Business Profile posts actually help with ranking?Yes. GBP posts signal to Google that your profile is active and relevant. Posts give Google fresh content with keywords to index, and profiles that post weekly see roughly 2x more engagement actions (calls, clicks, direction requests) than inactive profiles. Posts expire after 7 days, so consistency is required.
What is the difference between the local 3-pack and organic search results?The local 3-pack appears at the top of search results with a map and three business listings. It is driven by your Google Business Profile. Organic results appear below and are driven by your website’s SEO (content, backlinks, domain authority). You can rank in both, and the best strategy is to optimize both your GBP and your website simultaneously.
