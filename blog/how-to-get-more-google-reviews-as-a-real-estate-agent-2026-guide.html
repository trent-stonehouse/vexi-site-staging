How to Get More Google Reviews as a Real Estate Agent (2026 Guide)

TRENT STONEHOUSE12 MIN READMARCH 31, 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

To get more Google reviews as a real estate agent, aim for at least 25 reviews to hit the credibility threshold. Ask via text message instead of email (8x higher response rate), use a pre-written template with a direct link, and ask clients to include three keywords: your name, your city, and “real estate agent.” Then respond to every review by stuffing those same keywords into your reply. New agents can jumpstart with character reviews from people who know them personally.

Google reviews are the single most underused ranking tool in real estate. Most agents have fewer than 10. The ones dominating local search have 50+. The gap between those two agents isn’t talent or marketing budget. It’s a system.

This guide combines strategies from three proven approaches: why most agent reviews don’t actually help with rankings, the exact process for collecting reviews that move the needle, and how to turn every review response into an SEO asset. No fluff. Just the system.

98% of consumers read online reviews for local businesses, and Google reviews are the #1 factor in local search ranking. Real estate agents with 25+ reviews appear in the Google Local Pack 3x more often than agents with fewer than 10.

Sources: BrightLocal Consumer Review Survey, 2025 | Moz Local Search Ranking Factors, 2025

Why Google Reviews Matter More Than Ever for Real Estate

Google reviews are the new referral. Before a buyer or seller picks up the phone, they Google you. What they find in those first 3 seconds determines whether they call you or the agent below you.

Here’s what most agents miss: Google reviews don’t just build trust with consumers. They directly impact where you rank in local search results. Google’s algorithm weighs three factors for local rankings: relevance, distance, and prominence. Reviews are the biggest driver of prominence.

Google Local Pack

The Google Local Pack is the box of three business listings that appears at the top of Google search results with a map. For real estate agents, appearing in this box means showing up first when someone searches “real estate agent near me” or “realtor in [City].” Reviews are the #1 factor that determines which three agents appear here.

An agent with 47 reviews and a 4.9 rating will almost always outrank an agent with 6 reviews and a 5.0 rating. Volume beats perfection. Google wants to show businesses that lots of people have interacted with and validated.

Reviews also give Google keyword signals. When a client writes “John helped us buy our first home in Scottsdale,” Google now associates your profile with “buy,” “first home,” and “Scottsdale.” Every review is a micro SEO asset working for you 24/7.

Agents who actively manage Google reviews generate 35% more inbound leads than agents who rely solely on referrals. The compounding effect is real: more reviews lead to higher rankings, which lead to more clicks, which lead to more clients, which lead to more reviews.

Source: BrightLocal, 2025

The 25-Review Threshold: Your Minimum Target

25 reviews is the number where everything changes. Below that, you’re invisible in competitive local searches. Above it, Google starts treating your profile as a credible, established business.

This isn’t arbitrary. Data from Moz’s Local Search Ranking Factors study consistently shows that review quantity is one of the top three signals for Local Pack rankings. Agents in competitive markets often need 40-50+ to consistently rank, but 25 is the baseline where you start appearing.

The average real estate agent has 11 Google reviews. Getting to 25 puts you ahead of roughly 70% of agents in any given market. Getting to 50 puts you in the top 5-10%. The bar is low because most agents never build a system for collecting them.

Source: Moz Local Search Ranking Factors, 2025

The Math: How Fast Can You Get There?

If you close 2 deals per month and ask every client for a review, you’ll hit 25 in about a year. That’s too slow. Here’s how to accelerate it:

  • Ask both sides of the transaction — buyer and seller (if you represented both parties in different deals)
  • Ask referral partners — lenders, inspectors, and contractors you work with can leave professional reviews
  • Ask past clients — go back through your last 2 years of closings and send a text
  • Ask at the emotional peak — closing day, key handover, or the moment they get the news their offer was accepted

Most agents lose reviews because they wait too long. The best time to ask is within 24 hours of a positive milestone. The worst time is two weeks later when the excitement has faded.

How to Ask for Google Reviews (Without Being Awkward)

Text message beats email every time. Email review requests get a 5-10% response rate. Text messages get 40-50%. The reason is simple: people see texts immediately, and a direct link makes it a 60-second task.

Text messages have a 98% open rate compared to 20% for email. For review requests specifically, texts convert at 8x the rate of email. The key: include a direct link that opens your Google review form, not your Google Business profile.

Source: Gartner Digital Markets, 2025

Step-by-Step: The Review Request System

  1. Get your direct Google review link

    Go to your Google Business Profile, click “Ask for reviews,” and copy the short link. This link opens the review form directly — no searching or extra clicks for the client.

  2. Send a text within 24 hours of closing

    Strike while the emotion is high. Right after handing over keys or celebrating a successful close, send the pre-written text (templates below). Personalize it with their name and a specific detail about their experience.

  3. Give them the words to use

    Most clients stare at a blank review box and freeze. Tell them exactly what to mention: your name, your city, and what you helped them do. This removes friction and embeds SEO keywords in their review.

  4. Follow up once if no response

    Wait 3 days. Send one follow-up text. If they don’t respond after that, move on. Never pressure. One follow-up converts 30% of non-responders.

Pre-Written Review Request Templates

Copy these templates and save them in your phone. Swap the brackets for real details.

Template 1 — Post-Closing Text

Hey [Name]! Congrats again on closing on [Address] 🎉 I loved working with you. If you have 60 seconds, would you mind leaving me a Google review? It helps other [City] families find me. Here’s the link: [YOUR LINK]

If you mention that I helped you [buy your first home / sell in City / navigate a tough market], that would mean the world. 🙏

Template 2 — Past Client Re-Engagement

Hey [Name]! Hope you’re loving the house. I’m building up my Google reviews this year and would love your support. Would you mind leaving a quick review about your experience? Here’s the direct link: [YOUR LINK]

Even 2-3 sentences about working with [Your Name] as your [City] real estate agent would be amazing.

Template 3 — The Charity Incentive

Hey [Name]! For every Google review I receive this month, I’m donating $10 to [Local Charity]. Would you help me support [Charity] by leaving a quick review? Here’s the link: [YOUR LINK]

It takes 60 seconds and it goes to a great cause. Thank you!

The Charity Incentive Strategy

You can’t pay for reviews (that violates Google’s terms). But you can tie reviews to a charitable donation. “For every review I receive this month, I’ll donate $10 to [local charity].” This gives clients a reason beyond you to leave the review, dramatically increasing response rates while supporting your community.

The Holy Trinity of Review Keywords

Not all reviews are created equal for SEO. A review that says “Great agent, highly recommend!” does almost nothing for your Google ranking. A review that includes the right keywords tells Google exactly what you do and where you do it.

The three keywords every review should contain:

  1. Your full name

    “Sarah Johnson helped us…” — This associates your name with positive real estate experiences in Google’s knowledge graph.

  2. Your city or neighborhood

    “…buy our home in Scottsdale” — This is the geo-signal. It tells Google you serve that specific area and should rank for location-based searches.

  3. “Real estate agent” (or “realtor”)

    “She’s the best real estate agent we’ve worked with” — This is the service signal. It confirms your business category for search queries.

When a review contains all three — “Sarah Johnson is the best real estate agent in Scottsdale” — Google has everything it needs to rank you for “real estate agent Scottsdale,” “Scottsdale realtor,” and related searches.

“The agents ranking #1 in local search don’t have better marketing budgets. They have reviews full of the right keywords. Every review is a vote that tells Google who you are, what you do, and where you do it.”
— SEO principle from Moz Local Ranking Factors research

How to Get Keywords Into Reviews Without Being Pushy

You can’t dictate what clients write. But you can guide them. The templates above naturally prompt clients to mention your name, city, and service. Here’s another approach:

When you ask in person, say: “If you could mention that I helped you [buy/sell] in [City], that really helps other families find me on Google.” Most clients are happy to include those details because it’s a natural part of their story.

How to Respond to Reviews for Maximum SEO Impact

Your review responses are SEO gold that most agents waste. Google reads your responses and indexes the keywords in them. Every response is a chance to reinforce your name, location, and service keywords.

Most agents respond with “Thank you so much!” That’s a missed opportunity. Here’s what to do instead.

The Keyword-Rich Response Formula

Review Response Template

Thank you so much, [Client Name]! It was an absolute pleasure helping you [buy/sell] your home in [City/Neighborhood]. As a [City] real estate agent, nothing makes me happier than seeing [specific detail — e.g., “your family settle into the perfect home near [School/Park]”]. If anyone you know is looking for a real estate agent in [City], I’d love to help. Congratulations again! — [Your Name], [City] Real Estate Agent

Count the keywords in that response: your name (twice), your city (three times), “real estate agent” (twice), and a neighborhood reference. Every response is a mini SEO page working for you.

Go Back and Edit Old Responses

Here’s a tactic most agents don’t know: you can edit your past review responses. If you responded to your first 15 reviews with “Thanks!” or “Appreciate it!”, go back and rewrite them using the keyword-rich formula above.

  1. Open your Google Business Profile

    Go to your reviews section and find your oldest reviews with short, generic responses.

  2. Click “Edit” on your response

    Google lets you edit your responses at any time. The updated text gets re-indexed by Google’s crawlers.

  3. Rewrite using the keyword formula

    Replace “Thanks!” with a keyword-rich response that includes your name, city, service type, and a personal detail. Do 2-3 per day until you’ve updated all of them.

Businesses that respond to reviews are 1.7x more trustworthy in the eyes of consumers than businesses that don’t. And keyword-rich responses correlate with higher Local Pack rankings because Google indexes owner responses as additional page content.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, 2025

Google Reviews for New Agents: The Character Review Strategy

No closings yet? You can still build a strong review profile. New agents often think they need transactions before they can ask for reviews. That’s not true. Google doesn’t require that a reviewer be a paying client.

The strategy: character reviews. Ask people who know you personally and professionally to leave reviews about your character, work ethic, and trustworthiness.

Who Can Leave Character Reviews

  • Friends and family who can speak to your integrity, reliability, and work ethic
  • Past coworkers from previous careers who know your professionalism
  • Your broker or mentor who can vouch for your training and dedication
  • Vendors and partners — your lender, photographer, or stager who have worked alongside you
  • Community contacts — people you volunteer with, coach with, or serve alongside

Character Review Request Template

Hey [Name]! I just launched my real estate business in [City] and I’m building up my Google profile. Would you mind leaving me a review about what it’s like to work with me? You don’t have to be a client — just share your honest experience with my character and work ethic.

Here’s the direct link: [YOUR LINK]

If you could mention that I’m a real estate agent in [City], that would really help. Thank you!

Important: Keep It Authentic

Character reviews must be genuine and honest. The reviewer should clearly state their relationship to you (“I’ve known [Name] for 10 years and recently watched them launch their real estate career in [City]”). Fake reviews or reviews that misrepresent the relationship violate Google’s terms and can get your entire profile suspended. Authenticity is the goal.

With 10-15 character reviews, a new agent can launch their Google profile with enough social proof to compete with established agents in local search. Then layer transaction reviews on top as you close deals. Within 6 months, you’ll have a review profile that looks like a veteran agent’s.

Common Google Review Mistakes That Kill Your Rankings

Most agents sabotage their own Google review strategy without knowing it. Here are the mistakes to avoid.

Mistake Fix
Sending review requests via email only Always text first. Email as backup. Texts convert 8x better.
Linking to your Google profile instead of the review form Use the direct review link from “Ask for reviews” in your Google Business Profile.
Waiting weeks after closing to ask Ask within 24 hours of a positive milestone (closing, offer accepted, key handover).
Responding to reviews with “Thanks!” Use keyword-rich responses with your name, city, and “real estate agent.”
Never asking past clients Go back 2 years. Text every past client with a template and direct link.
Buying fake reviews Never. Google detects and penalizes fake reviews. One removal can wipe your entire profile.
Ignoring negative reviews Respond professionally, acknowledge the issue, offer to resolve it offline. Future clients read how you handle criticism.
Not having a system Save templates in your phone. Set a reminder to ask after every closing. Automate what you can.
