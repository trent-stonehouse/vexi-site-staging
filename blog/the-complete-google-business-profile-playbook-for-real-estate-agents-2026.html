What Is the Google Map Pack?

The Google Map Pack (also called the Local 3-Pack) is the section at the top of Google search results that shows a map with three local businesses. It appears for searches with local intent — like “real estate agent near me” or “realtor in Denver.” Getting into the Map Pack means appearing above all organic search results, which drives the majority of clicks for local searches.

The 5 Mechanisms That Control Your Google Ranking

Google uses five primary signals to decide which businesses appear in the Map Pack. Master all five, and you will outrank agents who only focus on one or two:

  1. Profile Completeness

    Google prioritizes profiles that are 100% filled out. Every empty field is a missed ranking signal. Name, categories, description, services, hours, attributes, photos — all of it matters.

  2. Reviews (Quantity + Quality + Recency)

    The number of reviews, your average rating, how recent they are, and what keywords appear in them. Google reads review text to understand what your business does and where.

  3. Review Responses

    Responding to every review — and including keywords in your responses — is a ranking signal most agents ignore completely. This is where the “Holy Trinity” technique comes in (covered in Step 6).

  4. GBP Posts

    Posting weekly on your Google Business Profile signals to Google that your business is active and relevant. Inactive profiles get buried. Active profiles get boosted.

  5. NAP Consistency (Name, Address, Phone)

    Your business name, address, and phone number must be identical everywhere they appear online — Google, Yelp, Zillow, Realtor.com, your website. Inconsistencies confuse Google and tank your ranking.

Step 1: Verify and Claim Your Profile

If you have not verified your Google Business Profile, nothing else in this guide matters. Verification is step zero. Without it, you cannot edit your listing, respond to reviews, or post updates. Google treats unverified profiles as unconfirmed — they rank significantly lower and may not show up at all.

Many agents already have a profile that Google auto-created from public data. The problem is that an unclaimed profile sits there collecting random information, potentially wrong hours, and reviews you can never respond to.

How to Verify Your Google Business Profile

  1. Go to business.google.com

    Sign in with your primary Google account — ideally the same one connected to your Google Ads and Analytics. Search for your business name. If it exists, click “Claim this business.” If not, click “Add your business.”

  2. Choose Your Business Category

    Select “Real estate agent” as your primary category. This is critical — your primary category is the strongest ranking signal Google uses to match you with searchers. You can add secondary categories later.

  3. Enter Your Service Area

    If you work from home or do not have a storefront, choose “I deliver goods and services to my customers” and list the cities/zip codes you serve. Do NOT use a P.O. box — Google will reject it.

  4. Complete the Verification Process

    Google will verify you by postcard (5–7 days), phone, email, or instant verification (if connected to Google Search Console). Postcard is the most common method for new profiles. Do not edit your profile name or address while the postcard is in transit — it will reset the process.

  5. Enter Your Verification Code

    Once you receive the postcard, log back in to business.google.com and enter the 5-digit code. Your profile is now live. Now the real optimization begins.

Verified Google Business Profiles are 70% more likely to attract location visits and 50% more likely to lead to a purchase. An unverified profile is invisible. A verified, optimized profile is a 24/7 lead generation machine.

Source: Google Business Profile Help Center

Step 2: Optimize Your Business Name

Your business name on Google is one of the strongest local ranking signals. Most agents list their name as “John Smith” or “Jane Doe Realty” and leave it at that. That is a missed opportunity.

Google allows you to include relevant keywords in your business name as long as they reflect your real-world business name. The key is staying within Google’s guidelines while maximizing your keyword exposure. If you stuff keywords unnaturally, Google will suspend your profile.

Good vs. Bad Business Names

Example Why It’s Bad Better Version
“John Smith” No keywords, no location, no context “John Smith — Austin Real Estate Agent”
“Best Realtor Austin TX Homes Buy Sell” Keyword stuffing — will get flagged “John Smith | Keller Williams Austin”
“Smith Realty Group” Missing city, missing service keyword “Smith Realty Group — Denver Real Estate”

Google’s Business Name Rule

Google’s official guideline states your business name should reflect the name your business uses in the real world. This means the name on your business cards, signage, and marketing materials. You can include a keyword and city if you genuinely use that in your branding — just make sure it matches your website, social profiles, and other listings exactly.

Pro tip: If your current branding does not include your city or service keyword, consider updating your overall branding to include it. Update your business cards, email signature, website header, and all directory listings to match. Then update Google. This is not gaming the system — it’s strategic branding.

Step 3: Fill Every Section of Your Profile

Google rewards completeness. A profile with every section filled out will outrank a profile with gaps — even if the incomplete profile has more reviews. Think of it like a resume: blank sections signal to Google that this business is not fully established or trustworthy.

Here is every section you need to complete, and exactly what to put in each one:

Primary Category

Set your primary category to “Real estate agent”. This is the most important category signal. Your primary category tells Google what searches to show you for. Do not use “Real estate agency” unless you are a brokerage — the “agent” category targets individual-level searches like “real estate agent near me.”

Secondary Categories

Add every relevant secondary category. These give Google additional context about your services. Recommended secondary categories for real estate agents:

  • Real estate agency — if you are part of a team or brokerage
  • Real estate consultant — for investor clients
  • Property management company — if you manage rentals
  • Real estate appraiser — only if you hold this certification
  • Real estate developer — only if applicable to your work

Business Description

You get 750 characters. Use every one of them. Your description should include your primary keyword, city/area, services, and what makes you different. Google reads this text for ranking signals.

Business Description Template

“[Your name] is a [specialty] real estate agent serving [city/area]. With [X] years of experience and [X] homes sold, [he/she] specializes in [specific services: first-time buyers, luxury homes, investment properties, relocations]. [Your name] provides [unique value: personalized market analysis, neighborhood expertise, full-service support from listing to closing]. Serving [list of neighborhoods/cities]. Contact [your name] today for expert [city] real estate guidance.”

Business Hours

Set your hours to reflect when you are genuinely available. Most successful agents use Monday–Friday 8:00 AM – 7:00 PM and Saturday 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM. Listing broad hours signals availability. Agents who show “Closed” on weekends miss Saturday searchers — which is the highest-traffic day for real estate searches.

Attributes

Google offers business attributes that appear on your profile. Check every one that applies:

  • Identifies as women-owned / veteran-owned / minority-owned (if applicable)
  • Online appointments available
  • Wheelchair accessible (if your office is)
  • Languages spoken

Attributes are not a ranking factor, but they improve click-through rates by making your profile more complete and relatable to searchers.

Photos

Upload at least 10–15 high-quality photos. Profiles with photos receive 42% more requests for directions and 35% more website clicks. Include:

  • Professional headshot (this becomes your profile photo)
  • Action shots of you with clients (with permission)
  • Photos of homes you have sold
  • Neighborhood and community photos
  • Your office or workspace
  • Team photos (if applicable)

Google Business Profiles with 100+ photos get 520% more calls than the average profile, and 2,717% more direction requests. Photos are not optional — they are one of the highest-ROI activities you can do on your GBP.

Step 4: The Services Section Keyword Hack

This is the single most underused ranking strategy on Google Business Profile. The Services section allows you to add an unlimited number of service descriptions. Each service description is indexed by Google and acts as a keyword signal. Most agents add 3–5 generic services and move on. Top-ranking agents add 30–50+.

Here is how it works: Google treats each service entry as a mini web page associated with your profile. The more relevant, keyword-rich services you list, the more search queries you become eligible to appear for.

How to Use Google Gemini to Generate Service Keywords

Instead of guessing which services to add, use Google’s own AI to generate them. Here is the exact process:

  1. Open Google Gemini (gemini.google.com)

    Sign in to your Google account and navigate to Gemini. This is Google’s AI — it understands what Google values for ranking because it is built by the same company.

  2. Use This Exact Prompt

    “Generate 50 real estate services that a real estate agent in [YOUR CITY], [STATE] would offer. Include services related to buying, selling, investing, first-time homebuyers, luxury homes, relocations, market analysis, and property types. Format each as a service title (5-8 words) followed by a one-sentence description (15-25 words) that includes the city name.”

  3. Review and Customize the Output

    Remove any services that do not apply to you. Edit the descriptions to sound natural and include your specific neighborhoods. Add any niche services you offer that Gemini missed.

  4. Add Each Service to Your GBP

    In your Google Business Profile dashboard, go to “Edit profile” → “Services.” Add each service with its title and description. There is no limit — add as many relevant services as you can.

  5. Update Monthly

    Add seasonal services (winter: “Holiday relocation assistance”), trending services (“AI home valuation for sellers”), and new neighborhood-specific services as you expand your farm areas. Fresh service entries signal ongoing activity to Google.

Step 5: Build Your Review Engine

Reviews are the most powerful ranking factor you can directly influence. Google weighs three things about your reviews: the total number, the average rating, and how recently they were posted. An agent with 50 reviews (4.9 stars, 5 posted this month) will outrank an agent with 200 reviews (4.8 stars, none posted in 6 months).

Here is the target: get to 25 reviews as fast as possible. Data shows that 25 is the threshold where Google starts taking your profile seriously for competitive local searches. Below 25, you are fighting an uphill battle. Above 25, your profile enters the consideration set for the Map Pack.

25 reviews is the minimum threshold for competitive Google Map Pack ranking. Agents with 25+ reviews appear in 3x more local searches than agents with fewer than 10. The difference between 10 reviews and 25 reviews is not linear — it is exponential in visibility.

Source: BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey, 2025

Why Text Messages Beat Email for Review Requests

Most agents send review requests via email. That is a mistake. Here is why:

Metric Email Text Message

Response Rate 1–3% 30–45%
Time to Respond Hours to days 90 seconds average
Feels Personal? No — feels automated Yes — feels like a real ask

The review request text message should be simple, personal, and include a direct link to your Google review page. Here is the process:

The Review Request System

  1. Get Your Google Review Link

    In your GBP dashboard, go to “Ask for reviews.” Copy the short link Google provides. This takes the reviewer directly to the review form — no searching required.

  2. Send a Text Within 24 Hours of Closing

    Timing matters. The client is happiest right after closing. Send this text: “Hey [Name], congratulations again on the new home! If you have 60 seconds, a Google review would mean the world to me. Here’s the link: [link]. Thank you!”

  3. Follow Up Once (If Needed)

    If they have not reviewed in 3 days, send one follow-up: “Hey [Name], no pressure at all — just wanted to resurface this in case it got buried. A quick Google review helps other families find me the same way you did. [link]” Then drop it. Never send more than two messages.

  4. Ask Character Witnesses (For New Agents)

    If you are a new agent with few or no transactions, ask for “character reviews” from people who know your work ethic: former clients from another industry, colleagues, mentors, lenders you have worked with. Google allows reviews from anyone who has had a business interaction with you — it does not have to be a closed deal.

The Charity Incentive Strategy

You cannot offer clients payment or discounts for reviews — Google prohibits it. But you can donate to charity. Here is a completely compliant approach that dramatically increases review rates:

The Charity Review Strategy

After closing, tell your client: “For every Google review I receive this month, I donate $10 to [local charity/food bank/school]. If you have a minute to leave a review, you would be helping a great cause.” This reframes the ask from “do me a favor” to “help the community.” Agents using this strategy report 2–3x higher review conversion rates compared to standard requests.

Building Reviews When You Are Brand New

New agents face a chicken-and-egg problem: you need reviews to get clients, but you need clients to get reviews. Here is how to solve it:

  • Character reviews from your sphere — People who know you professionally. Former coworkers, business contacts, and anyone you have helped with real estate advice (even informally).
  • Vendor and partner reviews — Title companies, lenders, inspectors, and other vendors you have worked with. They can review your professionalism and communication.
  • Mentor and coach reviews — If you have worked with a real estate coach or mentor, ask them to review your dedication and growth. This adds credibility.
  • Open house attendee reviews — After hosting an open house, follow up with attendees and ask for a review about your knowledge and helpfulness.

The goal is momentum. Your first 10 reviews are the hardest. After that, the social proof snowball effect kicks in — new clients see your reviews, hire you, and become your next reviewers.

Step 6: Write Keyword-Rich Review Responses

This is the ranking strategy that separates top agents from everyone else. Most agents either do not respond to reviews at all, or they respond with generic one-liners like “Thanks!” or “Appreciate the kind words!” That is a missed opportunity because Google reads your review responses for keyword signals.

Every review response is a chance to inject your target keywords — your name, your city, and your services — into content that Google associates with your profile.

The Holy Trinity of Review Responses

The Holy Trinity Formula

Every review response should include three elements: (1) The reviewer’s name, (2) your city or neighborhood, and (3) the service you provided. This turns a generic “thank you” into a keyword-rich ranking signal. Example: instead of “Thanks for the review!” you write a response that naturally mentions Austin, first-time homebuyer, and the specific neighborhood.

How to Respond to Negative Reviews

Negative reviews happen. How you respond matters more than the review itself. Potential clients are watching.

  1. Respond Within 24 Hours

    Speed signals that you take client satisfaction seriously. A delayed response looks like you are hiding or do not care.

  2. Stay Professional and Empathetic

    Never argue. Never get defensive. Start with empathy: “I’m sorry your experience did not meet expectations.” This is not about winning the argument — it is about showing future clients how you handle adversity.

  3. Take It Offline

    Offer to resolve the issue privately: “I’d love to discuss this further and make it right. Please reach out to me directly at [email/phone].” This shows you care without airing dirty laundry publicly.

  4. Still Include Your Keywords

    Even in a negative review response, weave in your location and service naturally: “As an Austin real estate agent, providing an excellent experience for every client is my top priority.”

Responding to reviews — both positive and negative — improves your local ranking by up to 15% according to local SEO studies. And 89% of consumers read business responses to reviews. Your response is as much for future clients as it is for the reviewer.

Source: BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey, 2025

Step 7: Post Weekly on Google Business Profile

Most agents do not even know you can post on Google Business Profile. But Google Business Posts are one of the strongest “activity signals” you can send. Posting tells Google that your business is active, relevant, and engaged. An inactive profile with no posts in 90+ days gets deprioritized. A profile that posts weekly stays in the algorithm’s good graces.

GBP posts appear directly on your Google profile when someone searches for you. They also appear in Google Maps and can show up in discovery searches. Think of them as free micro-ads that live on the most visited search engine in the world.

What to Post on Google Business Profile

  1. Market Updates

    Monthly or bi-weekly updates on your local market. Median home price, days on market, inventory levels, interest rate changes. This positions you as the local expert and includes natural keyword opportunities. Example: “Austin Real Estate Market Update — March 2026: Median home price rose 4.2% to $485,000. Inventory remains tight at 1.8 months of supply.”

  2. Just Sold / Just Listed

    Showcase your recent transactions. Include the neighborhood, property type, and a brief story. “Just sold this beautiful 4-bedroom home in Cedar Park for $15K over asking in just 5 days. The market is competitive, and my clients’ pricing strategy paid off.”

  3. Client Testimonial Highlights

    Turn your best reviews into posts. Screenshot the review or quote it with a photo of the happy clients (with permission). This creates a double ranking signal: the original review + a post that references it.

  4. Local Community Content

    Events, restaurant openings, school updates, neighborhood spotlights. This signals to Google that you are deeply connected to the local area — exactly the kind of agent searchers want to find.

  5. Educational Tips

    Short, actionable real estate tips. “3 things every first-time buyer in Denver should know before making an offer.” These attract engagement and keep your profile fresh. Include a CTA to contact you for personalized advice.

  6. Offers and Events

    Google has specific post types for “Offers” and “Events.” Use them. Free home valuations, open house announcements, first-time buyer workshops. These post types get special formatting and visibility in search results.

GBP Posting Best Practices

  • Post at least once per week — weekly is the minimum for maintaining activity signals. Twice per week is ideal.
  • Include a photo or image with every post — posts with images get 6x more engagement than text-only posts.
  • Add a call-to-action button — Google lets you add “Call now,” “Learn more,” “Book,” or “Sign up” buttons to every post.
  • Include your target keywords naturally — mention your city, neighborhoods, and services in the post text.
  • GBP posts expire after 6 months — plan to repost or refresh your best-performing content periodically.
