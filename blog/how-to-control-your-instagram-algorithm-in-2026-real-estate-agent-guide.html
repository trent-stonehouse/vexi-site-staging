How to Control Your Instagram Algorithm in 2026 (Real Estate Agent Guide)

TRENT STONEHOUSE15 MIN READMARCH 31, 2026

⚡ Quick Answer

You can control your Instagram algorithm by doing three things: reset your interests, study outlier content in your niche, and reproduce the formats that are already working. Instagram uses your behavior — what you watch, save, share, and search — to decide what it shows you and who it shows your content to. By deliberately training your own feed, you teach the algorithm what your niche looks like. Then you reverse-engineer the top-performing content in that niche to create posts that match proven formats. This is the fastest path to more reach for real estate agents on Instagram in 2026.

Most real estate agents blame the algorithm when their content underperforms. They assume Instagram is randomly suppressing their posts or playing favorites with bigger accounts.

That is not what is happening. The algorithm is a recommendation engine, and it is doing exactly what you trained it to do based on your own behavior on the platform.

The good news: you can retrain it. This guide walks through the exact three-step process to take control of your Instagram algorithm, stop getting irrelevant content, and start creating posts that the algorithm actually wants to push to your target audience.

Why Your Instagram Feed Feels Random (And How to Fix It)

Your Instagram feed feels random because you have been training the algorithm with random behavior. Every tap, scroll, save, and search tells Instagram what you care about. If you spend time watching cooking videos, car reviews, and celebrity gossip, Instagram assumes those are your interests — and it serves you more of the same.

For real estate agents, this creates a compounding problem. If your own feed is cluttered with content that has nothing to do with real estate, the algorithm does not categorize you as a real estate creator. That means your content gets shown to a mismatched audience.

Instagram’s recommendation engine powers over 40% of all content shown on the platform, up from roughly 15% in 2021. The algorithm increasingly relies on interest signals rather than follower relationships to decide what appears in your feed.

Source: Instagram Official — How Ranking Works

The fix is straightforward: you need to reset your interests and start giving Instagram clean, consistent signals about who you are and what niche you serve.

Think of it like this. If you walked into a bookstore and only browsed the cooking section for six months, the staff would stop recommending thrillers to you. Instagram works the same way, except it tracks every second of your attention and adjusts in real time.

The Two-Way Training Problem

Most agents only think about the algorithm in one direction — how it distributes their posts. But the algorithm also works in reverse. It uses your personal consumption habits to understand what category of creator you are.

When you spend 20 minutes scrolling through meme pages and fitness influencers, you are telling Instagram that your interests overlap with those audiences. Your content then gets tested against those audiences first, and it predictably underperforms because they are not interested in real estate.

Fixing this is the first step to controlling your algorithm, and it is something you can start doing today.

How the Instagram Algorithm Decides What You See

Instagram does not have one algorithm. It has multiple ranking systems, each tailored to a different part of the app. Feed, Stories, Explore, Reels, and Search each use their own set of signals to decide what content to surface.

Understanding these signals is critical because it tells you exactly what levers you can pull to influence what the algorithm does with your content.

Key Concept

Interest signals are the actions you take on Instagram that tell the algorithm what you care about. These include watching a video to the end, saving a post, sharing it via DM, searching for a topic, and following accounts in a specific niche. The algorithm weighs these signals to build a profile of your interests and match you with relevant content.

The Ranking Signals That Matter Most in 2026

Instagram has confirmed that the following signals carry the most weight in determining what content gets distributed:

  • Watch time: How long someone spends viewing your content. For Reels, watching to the end (or rewatching) is the strongest positive signal.
  • Saves: When someone bookmarks your post, it tells Instagram the content has lasting value.
  • Shares: Sending a post via DM or sharing to Stories indicates the content is worth spreading.
  • Follows after viewing: If someone follows you after seeing your content, that is the ultimate vote of confidence from the algorithm’s perspective.
  • Comments with depth: Longer comments carry more weight than single-emoji replies.

According to Hootsuite’s 2025 Social Media Trends report, shares and saves now generate 2–3x more algorithmic reach than likes and comments for business accounts.

Source: Hootsuite Social Media Trends Report

How the Algorithm Categorizes Your Account

Instagram assigns interest categories to every account based on the content it posts and the content its audience engages with. If you post a mix of real estate content, personal selfies, and random motivational quotes, the algorithm struggles to categorize you.

When the algorithm cannot categorize you clearly, it does not know which audiences to test your content with. The result is lower reach and inconsistent performance — which is exactly what most agents experience.

The agents who get consistent algorithmic reach have a clear content niche. Instagram knows exactly what they post, who should see it, and which audiences to test it with first.

Step 1: Reset Your Instagram Interests

Resetting your Instagram interests clears the slate so the algorithm stops showing you irrelevant content and starts recognizing you as a real estate niche creator. This is the foundation of the entire algorithm control strategy.

Instagram allows you to view and manage your interest preferences directly within the app. Most agents have never touched these settings, which means their algorithm profile is shaped by years of random browsing behavior.

How to Reset Your Interests

  1. Open Your Content Preferences

    Go to Settings → Content preferences → Suggested content. Here you will see the interest topics Instagram has assigned to you based on your activity.

  2. Remove Irrelevant Interests

    Delete every interest category that does not relate to real estate, housing, home design, local community content, or business growth. If it says “Cooking,” “Gaming,” or “Fitness” — remove it.

  3. Use “Not Interested” Aggressively

    For the next 7 days, tap the three-dot menu on every Explore page post that does not relate to real estate and select “Not interested.” This actively retrains the Explore algorithm.

  4. Deliberately Engage With Real Estate Content

    Search for real estate topics. Watch real estate Reels to completion. Save listing tours and market update posts. Follow 10–15 top-performing real estate creators in your market.

  5. Clean Up Your Search History

    Go to Settings → Your activity → Recent searches and clear anything unrelated to your niche. Then search for terms like “real estate tips,” “home buying 2026,” and your city name + real estate.

This process takes about 15 minutes, but its impact compounds over the following weeks. As Instagram receives consistent signals that you are a real estate creator who consumes real estate content, it begins categorizing your account more accurately and showing your posts to the right audiences.

Pro Tip

Do this on your business account, not your personal one. If you use the same device for both, make sure you are logged into the correct account when consuming real estate content. Instagram tracks behavior per account, not per device.

Step 2: Study Outlier Content in Your Niche

Outlier content is any post or Reel that dramatically outperforms the creator’s typical engagement. Studying these outliers reveals exactly what the algorithm is rewarding in your niche right now — not six months ago, not in theory, but today.

This is the research phase, and most agents skip it entirely. They create content based on gut instinct or whatever they saw someone else doing, without actually analyzing what is working in their specific niche and market.

How to Find Outlier Content

An outlier is a post that gets 5–10x the typical engagement or view count for that account. If an agent normally gets 2,000 views per Reel and one Reel hits 40,000, that is an outlier worth studying.

  • Browse the Explore page after resetting your interests. The algorithm should now be showing you real estate content. Look for posts with disproportionately high engagement relative to the account’s follower count.
  • Check competitors: Go to 10–15 real estate accounts in your market (or in similar markets). Sort their Reels by view count and identify which ones dramatically outperformed the rest.
  • Use free tools: Platforms like Sandcastles.ai assign outlier scores to posts, making it easy to spot content that broke through the algorithm.
  • Search trending audio: When a Reel uses trending audio and outperforms, note that audio. The algorithm pushes content that uses sounds gaining momentum.

Research from Sprout Social shows that Instagram Reels generate 67% more engagement than standard video posts, making them the most algorithm-friendly format for discovery in 2026.

Source: Sprout Social Instagram Statistics

What to Analyze in Each Outlier

When you find an outlier post, do not just watch it and move on. Break it down into components:

  • Hook (first 1–3 seconds): What did they say or show to stop the scroll? Write it down word for word.
  • Format: Is it a talking head, a text overlay walkthrough, a before/after, a tour, a listicle? Identify the structural format.
  • Length: How long is the video? Shorter Reels (15–30 seconds) often outperform longer ones for discovery.
  • Caption structure: Does it lead with a question, a bold statement, or a keyword-rich description?
  • Audio: Is it trending audio, original audio, or a voiceover?
  • CTA: What action does the creator ask for — save, share, comment, follow?

Document at least 10–15 outliers before moving to Step 3. The patterns will become obvious: certain hooks, formats, and topics consistently outperform in your niche.

Step 3: Reproduce Successful Formats

Reproducing successful formats is not copying — it is using proven structures and adapting them to your market, brand, and audience. The algorithm already validated these formats by pushing them to large audiences. Your job is to apply the same structural elements to your own content.

This is where most agents get stuck. They see a viral Reel and think they need to recreate it exactly. That is the wrong approach. You want to extract the format, not clone the content.

The Format Extraction Framework

Take your list of 10–15 outlier posts and group them by format type. You will typically find 3–4 dominant formats:

  1. The Bold Claim Hook: Opens with a controversial or surprising statement. Example format: “Stop doing [common practice] — here is what works instead.”
  2. The Local Authority: Content that positions the creator as the expert in a specific area. Example format: “3 things buyers need to know about [neighborhood].”
  3. The Process Reveal: Shows a behind-the-scenes process that demystifies real estate. Example format: “Here is exactly what happens after your offer gets accepted.”
  4. The Data Drop: Leads with a specific number or stat. Example format: “Home prices in [city] changed by X% this month — here is what that means for you.”

How to Adapt Without Copying

  1. Keep the Structure, Change the Content

    If a viral post used a “3 mistakes buyers make” format, create your own version about your specific market. Same structure, different substance.

  2. Match the Pacing

    If the outlier Reel had a fast-paced editing style with text overlays every 2 seconds, replicate that pacing. The algorithm rewards content that keeps watch time high.

  3. Use Similar (Not Identical) Hooks

    The hook is the most important element. Study the pattern of the hook, then rewrite it for your niche. “Nobody talks about this” works across topics — just change what follows.

  4. Localize Everything

    The algorithm favors content that matches local interest signals. Mention your city, neighborhood, or region by name. This helps Instagram match your content to nearby users.

The key insight here is that the algorithm has already told you what it wants to push. By studying what went viral and reproducing the structural elements, you are working with the algorithm instead of against it.

According to a Social Insider study, Instagram Reels with text overlays see 30% higher completion rates than Reels without them — a direct signal that boosts algorithmic distribution.

How Real Estate Agents Should Train Their Algorithm

Training your algorithm means creating a consistent feedback loop between your consumption habits, your content niche, and your audience’s behavior. For real estate agents specifically, this requires intentional daily actions that most agents never think about.

Daily Algorithm Training Habits

Spend 10–15 minutes each day on these actions. Do them from your business account:

  • Watch 5–10 real estate Reels to completion. Do not scroll past them halfway. Watch time is one of the strongest interest signals.
  • Save 2–3 posts from real estate creators. This tells Instagram you want more content like this.
  • Search for real estate keywords. Search terms like “homes for sale [your city],” “real estate market update,” and “first-time homebuyer tips.”
  • Comment on 3–5 posts from agents in your market. Leave thoughtful comments, not just emojis. This creates relationship signals that the algorithm uses to connect your accounts.
  • Share 1–2 real estate posts to your Stories. Sharing behavior is one of the strongest signals for both your interests and your audience’s interests.

Content Posting Strategy for Algorithm Alignment

What you post matters as much as what you consume. The algorithm cross-references your posting history with your engagement patterns to build a more complete picture of your niche.

Algorithm Alignment Rule

Post content that matches what you consume. If you spend your time watching and saving market update videos, post market update videos. This creates a consistent signal loop that tells Instagram exactly who you are and who your audience should be.

For real estate agents, the strongest content categories for algorithm alignment are:

  1. Local market data and updates — positions you as the authority in your area
  2. Buyer/seller education — drives saves and shares (the top algorithm signals)
  3. Behind-the-scenes process content — creates curiosity and watch time
  4. Property tours and walkthroughs — naturally engaging and location-specific
  5. Client success stories — social proof that builds trust and drives follows

What to Avoid

Some behaviors actively confuse the algorithm and hurt your reach:

  • Do not post off-topic content from your business account. Save personal content for your personal profile.
  • Do not use generic hashtags like #realestate or #realtor. Use location-specific and niche-specific hashtags, or better yet, focus on keywords in your captions.
  • Do not engage with content outside your niche from your business account. Every like, comment, and watch sends a signal.
  • Do not delete and repost content. The algorithm treats deleted content as a negative signal.

The 30-Day Algorithm Reset Plan

This 30-day plan walks you through the complete algorithm reset process, from clearing your old interests to producing content that the algorithm actively promotes. Follow it in order. Each week builds on the previous one.

Week 1: Clean and Reset (Days 1–7)

  1. Day 1–2: Clear Your Interests

    Go to content preferences and remove all non-real-estate interests. Clear your search history. Unfollow accounts outside your niche from your business profile.

  2. Day 3–7: Retrain With “Not Interested”

    Spend 10 minutes daily on the Explore page marking irrelevant content as “Not Interested.” Simultaneously, watch real estate Reels to completion and save 3–5 posts per day.

Week 2: Research and Study (Days 8–14)

  1. Day 8–10: Identify 15 Outlier Posts

    Browse your now-retrained Explore page and competitor accounts. Identify 15 posts that dramatically outperformed their account average. Save them to a collection called “Outlier Research.”

  2. Day 11–14: Analyze and Document Patterns

    Break down each outlier using the analysis framework (hook, format, length, caption, audio, CTA). Identify the 3–4 dominant formats in your niche.

Week 3: Create and Test (Days 15–21)

  1. Day 15–17: Create 3 Posts Using Outlier Formats

    Pick the top 3 formats you identified and create one post for each. Adapt the structure to your market, use local keywords, and match the pacing and hook style of the originals.

  2. Day 18–21: Post and Measure

    Post one piece per day. Track views, saves, shares, and follows for each. Continue your daily algorithm training habits (watching, saving, searching real estate content).

Week 4: Optimize and Scale (Days 22–30)

  1. Day 22–25: Double Down on What Worked

    Review your Week 3 performance data. Whichever format got the best saves-to-views ratio, create 3 more posts using that same format with different topics.

  2. Day 26–30: Build Your Posting Rhythm

    Establish a consistent posting schedule (3–5 posts per week). Continue daily algorithm training. By Day 30, your Explore page should look completely different — and your reach should be measurably higher.

According to NAR’s 2024 Digital Age report, 52% of real estate agents acquired clients through social media in the past year, up from 47% the year before — making algorithm mastery directly tied to revenue growth.

After 30 days, you should see a noticeable shift in three areas: your Explore page shows mostly real estate content, your reach per post has increased, and your follower quality has improved (more local homebuyers and sellers, fewer random followers).

This is not a one-time fix. It is an ongoing practice. The agents who maintain these habits month after month are the ones who build sustainable algorithmic reach that translates into actual leads and closings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to reset the Instagram algorithm?Most users see a noticeable shift in their Explore page within 7–10 days of consistent interest resetting. Full algorithmic recalibration — where your content starts getting pushed to better-matched audiences — typically takes 3–4 weeks of daily training habits combined with niche-consistent posting.
Can I control the Instagram algorithm without posting Reels?You can influence it with carousels and static posts, but Reels are the highest-reach format on Instagram in 2026. The algorithm prioritizes video content for discovery (Explore and Suggested). If you want maximum algorithmic reach, Reels should be part of your content mix. Carousels are a strong second option for saves and shares.
What is an outlier post and how do I find one?An outlier post is any piece of content that gets 5–10x more views or engagement than the creator’s typical performance. Find them by scrolling through competitor accounts and sorting their Reels by view count, or use tools like Sandcastles.ai that calculate outlier scores automatically. Look for posts where the view count is dramatically higher than the account’s average.
Does engaging with other agents’ content help or hurt my algorithm?It helps, as long as those agents are in your niche. Engaging with real estate content from your business account reinforces your niche signal. Comment on posts from agents in your market, share their content to your Stories, and save posts that demonstrate the content style you want to create. The algorithm uses these relationship signals to better categorize your account.
Should I clear my followers to reset the algorithm?No. Clearing followers (removing people who do not engage) is an aggressive tactic that can backfire. Focus on training your algorithm through consumption behavior and content consistency instead. Over time, the algorithm will naturally prioritize showing your content to the segment of your followers who actually engage, and it will use their interest signals to find new audiences that match.
Is reproducing someone else’s content format considered copying?No. Reproducing a format means using the same structural elements — hook style, pacing, caption structure — while creating completely original content for your market. Every successful creator studies what works and adapts proven formats. The key is to change the substance (your market data, your expertise, your local angle) while keeping the structural framework that the algorithm has already validated.
